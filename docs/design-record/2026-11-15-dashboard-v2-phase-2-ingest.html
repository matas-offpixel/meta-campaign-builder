Dashboard v2 phase 2: the words for audience breakdowns and direct-to-fan stats

This record amends 2026-11-13-dashboard-v2-phase-2.html (E1, E5, EV1, EV4), which stands except where it's named here. It answers the questions two data-layer PRs raised: #1244 (HE1, audience breakdowns) and #1243 (HE3, direct-to-fan stats). Neither PR has a UI, so these rulings are what the panel build follows. No new frames.

What the dry runs found, checked against ad_daily_insights on 15 Nov:

  • Spend agrees within pennies, not to the penny. Locklead's breakdowns hold £1,569.47 against the campaigns' £1,569.52 for 1 Sep–14 Nov. On Sale differs by 6p, from rounding across many age × gender rows.
  • Signups don't quite agree: 500 in the breakdowns against 498 on the campaigns. Purchases agree at 25.
  • Meta returns spend by region, but no signups or purchases by region.

Rulings

#Ruling
PI1 · The age table's totals
  • The All row is the sum of the age rows, so the table always adds up.
  • Spend is shown in whole pounds, as EV1 draws it, so a few pence of rounding across segments never shows as a mismatch.
  • When the rows add to a different signup or purchase count than the campaigns, one line goes under the table's footnote. It names only the measures that differ:

    "Meta models its split by age, so these add to {a} signups and {p} purchases; the campaigns count {A} and {P}."

    For Locklead today that's "… so these add to 500 signups; the campaigns count 498."
  • When spend differs by more than £1, the table isn't shown. In its place: "Meta's age figures haven't caught up with its campaign spend yet. They'll show after the next nightly read." That would be a data fault, not modelling.
  • Every age Meta reports is a row, 65+ included. EV1 left it out only for the frame's width. "Age unknown" is the last row, never spread across the others.
  • The CTR column is link clicks ÷ impressions, headed "Link CTR" in place of EV1's "CTR". Link clicks are the step towards a signup. All clicks stay stored.
  • Gender is stored, not drawn. EV1 has no gender table. It needs a frame before it shows.
PI2 · Region rows

Region is its own small table, not extra rows in the age table:

  • Its title is "Meta delivery by region · Meta says", and its columns are Region, Spend, Impr. share and Link CTR.
  • It has no result columns. There's no "—" in a Signups cell and no "not reported" per row. A dash reads as "none happened", and a per-row note repeats itself.
  • One line sits under it: "Meta doesn't report signups or sales by region."
  • The rows come in a fixed order: England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, then anything else by spend, then "Region unknown". That's the same order as TikTok's UK nations.
PI3 · The panel model (#1244's list)

The WhoWeReached model in #1244's body is accepted, with these changes:

  • ctrText becomes linkCtrText, on Meta's rows and region rows (PI1).
  • metaSays gains totalsNote (PI1's line, or null) and withheld (PI1's over-£1 case).
  • regionNote is exactly PI2's line.
  • byGender stays in the model and isn't drawn (PI1).
  • TikTok keeps E1's sentence, "TikTok reports clicks by age, not signups or sales". Its CTR is TikTok's clicks ÷ impressions, headed "CTR", because TikTok has no link-click split here.
  • The empty states are accepted: "No Meta delivery by age yet" and "No TikTok delivery by age yet".
PI4 · Raw open rate
  • The raw open rate is never shown, on the client view or internally. It's stored only to work out the "est." ratio (E5).
  • The reason: the 13 Nov Robbie Doherty send had 17 bot-filtered opens of 572 (3.0 %) against a raw 28.7 %. Showing the two side by side invites exactly the comparison E5 rules out.
  • The email row keeps EV4's label, "bot-filtered", with "(est.)" only on rows that are estimated.
  • The table's footnote gains: "Email opens are Mailchimp's bot-filtered opens: Apple Mail Privacy and link-scanner opens removed."
PI5 · WhatsApp cost before the rate is set

Until the client's rate is set, the cost is blank, never £0:

  • Internal view: Cost reads "Rate not set" in the muted ink, linking to the client's rate field, and £ / click reads "—".
  • Share link: "—" in both.
  • The headline still compares click rates, which don't need a cost.

"—" alone on the internal view would read like email's "—", which means "not per send". This is a different case: missing config.

PI6 · Whole-list and unattributed sends
  • A show's figures never include a whole-list send, as E5 rules.
  • Client view: whole-list sends count in the channel totals. Each one is listed with "Whole list" in the Show column, and the totals line ends "· includes {n} whole-list sends".
  • Venue view: a whole-list send counts only when its audience or list belongs to that venue, for example NX Newcastle's Mailchimp audience. Otherwise it appears on the client view only.
  • Unattributed sends are on an account shared by two clients, such as GMC and Throwback's Bird workspace. They're on no client's dashboard. Internally there's one review line: "{n} sends on a shared account couldn't be tied to a client."
PI7 · Ticket holders vs marketing

#1243's rule is accepted as written:

  • A send is "ticket holder" when a targeted list or tag name contains "ticket holders", "ticketholders", "purchasers", "buyers" or "bookers". Everything else is marketing.
  • A door-times send to the master list stays marketing, because the audience decides, not the subject.
