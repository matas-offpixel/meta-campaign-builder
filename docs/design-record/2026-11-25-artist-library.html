Off/Pixel · design record · artist library (AL) · 25 November 2026 · before CC PR 1

An artist’s best material, kept once and found every time

The spec is scratch/artist-library/plan.md. This record gives the words and frames for the four places the library shows up:

  • the artist page, /artists/[id];
  • the “worked with before” card in the event page’s Creative Lab;
  • the Drive library, 00 Artist Library / {Artist} /;
  • get_artist’s answer to Claude.

The rights rule, as Matas confirmed it on 25 Nov: promoter-filmed footage stays with the promoter. Only the artist’s own material, press kits, and what Off/Pixel builds from them travel between clients. What we’ve learned travels too, but never with another client’s name on it.

All four surfaces are for operators today. No client sees any of them. So the operator words name the owning client, and a second set of words (.public) is recorded for any future surface a client can see.

Rulings

#Ruling
AL1 · The rights badge
  • Every file, caption and copy line in the library carries one badge:
    • Shareable (olive): it can be used for any client.
    • This client only · {client} (brick): it stays with that client. The client’s name is exactly as stored.
  • A brick badge always has a reason under it, the first of these that applies:
    • “Filmed by {client}” (owner_kind promoter);
    • “Uses {client}’s footage” (a render with any promoter clip in it);
    • “Has {client}’s branding or artwork”;
    • “Names {client}’s venue or brand” (a caption or copy line).
  • A shareable badge has no reason under it. Off/Pixel’s own copy is shareable unless it names the client’s venue, brand or show.
  • For the owning client’s own events, its material counts like shareable material. It is listed, ranked and usable, and keeps its brick badge, so nobody mistakes it for something that travels.
  • Anywhere a client could see it (no such surface yet):
    • the badge reads “This client only”, with no name;
    • reasons say “the promoter”;
    • another client’s event code reads “A past show”;
    • another client’s material isn’t listed at all.
  • There’s no override on screen. “Both clients say yes” isn’t built, so promoter material never travels until it is.
AL2 · The artist page
  • Header:
    • the artist’s name;
    • “{n} shows · {m} clients · {k} shareable files”, each count with its own one-form;
    • “Drive folder ↗”, or “No Drive folder yet: it’s made once {artist} is on an upcoming show.”
    Shows counts only shows with spend on the artist’s ads. A show still to come is listed but not counted.
  • Shows, newest first. Columns: Show (code and name), Client, Date, Phase, “On {artist}’s ads”, Results.
    • Spend is on ads matched to the artist only, never the whole show’s spend, because one lineup show can carry ten artists.
    • Results counts signups and purchases from those ads.
    • A show still to come reads “Coming up” in Results.
    • A code found only in ad names (NX26-ENZO) gets a row too. In place of date and phase it reads “Not an event in Off/Pixel: found from ad names, Aug–Oct 2026”.
  • Best for {stage}, one block per stage that has ranked ads, Signup then On sale:
    • stages are the tree’s stage words (RC14), and an Unsorted ad isn’t ranked;
    • a stage block shows up to four tiles, best first, and every client’s material is in the ranking;
    • each tile carries a thumbnail, its kind (Clip, Static, Render), the file’s own name in full, “{code} · {stage}”, its cost per result, and the badge;
    • the ranking uses the shrunk figure; the tile shows the actual one;
    • the note under the block reads “Ranked by cost per result across every client’s shows. An ad under £{min} spent isn’t ranked.”
  • Other files: shareable clips and statics that haven’t run, such as raw clips and press shots. Same tile, with no figure.
  • Captions:
    • “In {artist}’s voice, approved”: each caption in quotes, exactly as it ran, never retyped or corrected. Under it, “Approved by {approver}, {date} · logged by {operator}”, and the badge. A caption without an approval isn’t in the library.
    • “Copy that worked”: each line with “{stage} · {cost} over {n} ads · {code}”, and the badge.
  • What we’ve learned: AL3’s lines, then “Built overnight · last {date}”. Four kinds of line:
    • best: “{stage}: “{name}” did best, {cost} over {n} ads.”;
    • audience: “Audience: {audience} did best for {stage}, {cost} on {code}.”;
    • didn’t work: “Didn’t work: {what} at {stage}, {cost}, {x}× the stage’s usual.”;
    • not measured: “Not measured: {what} on {code}. {ticketing}’s checkout has no pixel, so it shows nothing, not a failure.” This keeps an untracked audience from reading as a failed one, as RA’s interest targeting did on NX26-ENZO.
  • 1280 puts What we’ve learned and Captions in a right-hand column. 393 stacks them: header, Shows as cards, Best for, Other files, What we’ve learned, Captions. Tiles go four across at 1280 and two across at 393.
AL3 · The card in Creative Lab
  • One card per artist on the event’s lineup who has played another show we worked on, in lineup order. An artist with no history gets no card.
  • Title: “We’ve worked with {artist} on {n} shows”, or “on 1 show”. The count leaves out this event. Under the title are the codes, newest first.
  • “Best for {stage}”: the round’s stage, where the Lab’s upcoming and signup map to Signup and on_sale to On sale. Under it, the top three that can be used on this event: shareable material, plus this client’s own. Tiles are the same as AL2’s.
  • Another client’s material that would have ranked is counted in one line: “1 more from Puzzle’s shows stays with Puzzle.” (or “{n} more … stay …”). It is never drawn.
  • Use in this round adds the tiles shown to the Lab round as a starting point. The button gives way to “Added 3 to this round.” (or “Added 1 to this round.”), and each tile reads “In this round”. Nothing goes to Meta.
  • “Open {artist}’s page” links to AL2.
  • When the newest usable file is over 6 months old: “The newest of these is from {month year}, over 6 months ago. Check Instagram for anything newer.”
  • When everything is another client’s (the asked case):
    • no tiles and no Use;
    • “Everything we hold from that show was filmed or branded by {client}, so it stays with {client} and can’t go into this round.” (or “from those shows”);
    • “What we learned there still applies.” with the page link, because insights travel;
    • “Start from {artist}’s Instagram or press kit.”
AL4 · Drive names
  • Folders:
    • the root folder is “00 Artist Library”;
    • under it, one folder per artist, named exactly as artists.name (verbatim, never case-folded or tidied);
    • in each artist folder: “Clips”, “Statics”, “Renders”, “Captions”, “Insights”.
  • The library holds shareable material only. A client’s own material stays in that client’s event folder, and the artist page and get_artist point to it there. The library is what any thread can lift without asking.
  • Clips and Statics keep each file’s own name, never renamed.
  • Renders holds shortcuts, never copies, named “{rank} · {code} · {stage} · {cost} · {file name}”, the file name without its extension, for example “01 · P26-BDAY · Signup · £2.10 per signup · Enzo Siragusa - UGC 2”.
    • The rank is two digits, counts only shareable renders, and runs per stage. That puts each stage’s best at the top of Drive’s name sort.
    • The top five per stage are kept. The weekly run renames a shortcut whose rank or figure changed, and removes one that drops out. Removing a shortcut never touches the file in the event folder.
  • Captions holds one doc, “{artist} · Captions”, under three headings:
    • “Shareable”;
    • “Only for {client}”, one per client: this one exception names the client, because the doc is Off/Pixel only;
    • “Copy that worked”.
  • Insights holds one doc, “{artist} · Insights”. Its first line is “What we’ve learned about {artist} · built {date}”, then “Off/Pixel only. The numbers come from every client’s shows. Never name one client to another.”, then AL2’s lines.
  • Nobody outside Off/Pixel gets these folders: Share with promoter can’t reach the library.
AL5 · get_artist’s answer
  • Plain text in AL2’s order: shows, best per stage, other files, captions, copy, insights. Every file line carries its badge and a Drive link. Names stay whole.
  • It takes an optional event code.
    • With one, it lists shareable material plus that client’s own, and counts another client’s in one line, the card’s held line in its own words: “Not listed: 3 more from Puzzle’s shows, which stay with Puzzle.”
    • Without one: “No event given, so only shareable material is listed.”
  • Before the shows, a rights line Claude can’t miss: “Insight numbers can be used. Don’t name {other} or its shows in anything {client} sees.”
  • It ends with the Instagram rule: “Newest material: {month year}. Look on Instagram only for what’s missing here.”, or the over-6-months form.
  • No match: “No artist called “{name}” in Off/Pixel.”, then “Closest: {names}.”
AL6 · Counts, money, dates
  • Every count has a one-form row.
  • Money uses £ with thousands separators. Cost per result has two decimals; spend is whole pounds.
  • Dates use the campaign creator’s forms (“Sun 27 Dec 2026”, and “Wed 25 Nov” within this year). A month is written “Oct 2026”.
  • Event codes, domains and “…{last 4}” never break across lines (RC rule). File names wrap at spaces, but are never shortened.
