RT11 · Step heights

The campaign's total is a share of current daily spend (RT4's: the show's 7-day average Meta spend per day, Tests and Retargeting left out). The share depends on the stretch (Matas, 17 Nov):

StretchShare of current daily spend
Normally10 %
Payday stretch15 % (RT6's window: 18 days from the Thursday before payday)
Final month15 % (from 30 days out)
Final two weeks20 % (from 14 days out)
Final week25 % (from 7 days out, to the show day)
  • Overlaps: the higher share applies; they never add up. A payday stretch inside a run-in step at 15 % or more adds nothing.
  • Holds and cuts still apply (Matas). RT5's frequency 4 hold and frequency 6 cut work during a step as at any other time.
  • Create starts at today's share. RT4's 10 / 15 / 20 % choice is withdrawn: a show 10 days out starts at 20 %. RT4's split, floor, money and no-budget sentences stand.
  • Each step is a line, proposed at the read the day before it starts, so it can be accepted before the day. Its target is the share × current daily spend at that read. The rise is the target minus today's total. There's no line when today's total is already at or above the target.
  • Splitting the rise (sharpens RT6's "split by room"):
    • A tier at frequency 4 or more doesn't rise.
    • The rise is split among the other tiers in proportion to their budgets.
    • A tier that reaches its room stops there, and what's left is split again among the rest.
    • Money no tier can take isn't raised.
  • Dates: a step runs from its first day to the day before the next higher share begins. The final week runs to the show day. A payday stretch outside the run-in ends on its last day, and Meta's budget schedule returns the tiers to their budgets.
  • Money: RT6's order stands. A step holds the rise × its days against the plan when it's accepted, from headroom first, then on-sale campaigns, inside R5's 20 % cap.
  • Steps are switched on behind the same gates as the rest of the panel.

Words.

  • Create line, first line: "£{total} a day · {share} % of current daily spend · Bottom £{b} · Mid Bottom £{mb} · Mid £{m}". Inside a step, the share reads "{share} % of current daily spend, {stretch}".
  • Create line, new third line, listing only steps still to come: "Steps: {share} % from {Ddd d Mon} · … · each held when it's accepted".
  • Step line: Step up · "{Payday | Final month | Final two weeks | Final week} · {Ddd d Mon} – {Ddd d Mon}" · "£{from} → £{to} a day · {share} % of current daily spend (£{spend}) · {tier} £{a} → £{b}[, its room] · … · {tier} doesn't rise (frequency {f})" · "Budget schedule on each tier ad set · £{hold} to {Ddd d Mon} · {from plan headroom | £{x} a day from {campaign}}".
  • Step line on a phone: "{stretch} · {Ddd d} – {Ddd d Mon}" · "£{from} → £{to} a day · {share} % · {from}".
  • When no tier can rise, a note with nothing to accept: "{stretch} step held: every tier is at frequency 4 or more, so nothing rises."
  • Panel header inside a step: "£{total} a day · {stretch}: {share} % of daily spend on {Ddd d Mon}" (the day the step began). Outside a step, RT3's "{pct} % of current daily spend" stands.
  • Footer: "Next steps: {share} % from {Ddd d Mon}, {stretch} · …". A payday stretch is listed only where it's the higher share.
RT12 · Buyers from ticketing

Yes, per client (Matas). It's switched on only once that client confirms their privacy notice or ticketing terms cover advertising use. Until then, buyers come only from hand-uploaded Buyers lists.

  • The setting: "Buyers from ticketing" on the client page (frame RF). It's off for every client. Turning it on needs four fields:
    • Covered by: Privacy notice or Ticketing terms;
    • Where it says so: a link or the document's name;
    • Confirmed by: name and role at the client;
    • Confirmed on: the date.
    Only Matas can switch it on. The fields are kept with who entered them and when.
  • Its words: "Off · {client} hasn't confirmed their privacy notice or ticketing terms cover advertising use." with no fields filled; "Off · confirmation recorded, ready to switch on." with all four; "On since {Ddd d Mon}" with the fields and "Entered by {name} · {Ddd d Mon}, {HH:mm}" under it. "Switch on" is disabled until all four fields are filled, with "Only Matas can switch this on."
  • What it does: every day before the 08:00 read, it takes each of the show's ticketing connections that can list a show's buyers and uploads those buyers to one customer list per show: {code} · Buyers · ticketing, role Buyers, email and phone only. Every tier leaves it out, alongside any hand-uploaded Buyers list.
  • Switching it off stops the uploads. At the next read the ticketing list is taken out of every tier's leave-outs and deleted from the ad account. Hand-uploaded lists stay.
  • Gates: the client's setting, plus the audience-write gate.

Buyers lines (amends RT8), under the tier table:

  • Off, client has a ticketing connection: RT8's "No buyers list yet, so buyers aren't left out and will keep seeing these ads." with "Upload a buyers list", then in ink-3: "Buyers from {connection} can be left out automatically once {client} confirms their privacy notice or ticketing terms cover advertising use." Action: "Record the confirmation" (the client page).
  • Off, no connection: RT8 as it stands.
  • On and updated: "Buyers from {connection} · {n} sent · updated {Ddd d Mon}, {HH:mm}". The tier rows' leave-outs say "buyers".
  • On, but the connection can't list buyers: "{connection} can't list this show's buyers, so upload a buyers list by hand." with "Upload a buyers list".
  • On, today's update failed: "Buyers from {connection} couldn't be updated {Ddd d Mon}: {reason}. The list from {Ddd d Mon} is still left out." From 7 days old, RT8's " · {n} days old" is added.
  • A hand-uploaded list shows its own RT8 line beside any of these.
  • Phone: "Buyers from {connection} · {n} · {Ddd d Mon}".
RT13 · The show's own links, from CIRQLIN
  • The source: CIRQLIN's partner read, GET /api/partner/page-links?crm_base_tag={tag}, with the show's tag. It returns every published page for the tag, event pages and crowd pages alike. Each page carries its landing_url and ticket_links[] (platform, url, label, shop_name, shown_first), exactly as the public page shows them.
  • Every link is a URL rule (Matas): every page's landing_url, and every ticket link on every page, one rule per link.
    • Each rule is "URL contains" the link from its host to the end of its path, exactly as returned: no scheme or query string, no case-folding, nothing trimmed.
    • For the Robbie Doherty show, that's robbiedoherty-newcastle.com plus a rule each for Skiddle, RA, Fatsoma, Fixr and DICE.
  • Which audience each link feeds:
    • Ticket links feed ticket-link visitors and checkout starters. Checkout starters are the pixel's Initiate Checkout on a ticket link's rule, never pixel-wide, because a shared pixel's checkouts belong to other shows too.
    • Landing pages feed page visitors and registrations (RT15).
  • Daily read: the links are read again at every 08:00 read and compared exactly with the rules. Any difference rewrites that audience's rules (the audience-write gate), so an outlet switched off on CIRQLIN drops out at the next rebuild and a new one comes in. If the read fails on a running campaign, the rules stay as they were and the read tries again the next day.
  • Has it sent visits? A third-party ticket page counts visitors only when the promoter's pixel is on that platform. So for each link the panel checks whether the resolved pixel recorded a visit matching its rule in the last 28 days, read by URL.
  • Names in the tier row (Matas):
    • A ticket link is named by its platform, as CIRQLIN returns it (Skiddle, RA, Fatsoma, Fixr, DICE).
    • Two links on one platform are told apart by their shop_name, else their label, in brackets: "Skiddle (Early bird)".
    • A landing page is named by its host and path: "xmpl-whse.com", "crqln.com/xmpl-whse-crowd".
    • The link shown first goes first, then the page's order.
  • The line, under the tier's audiences, in ink-3:
    • "Visits from {links}", with " · none from {links} in 28 days" when some have none.
    • When any ticket link has none, a second line: "A ticket site sends visits only when your pixel is on it there."
    • When no link of the tier has any: "No visits from {links} in 28 days".
    • Phone: "Visits: {links} · none from {links}".
    Bottom names its ticket links. Mid Bottom names its landing pages.
  • Empty Bottom: with no ticket-link visits, no checkout starters, no registrations and no signup list, Bottom can't be built: "Bottom has no audience yet: no visits, checkout starters, registrations or signup list." Its 50 % then goes to Mid Bottom and Mid, 1 to 1.
  • Not proposed, replacing the Create line (RD-393 adds both):
    • 404 no_page: "Retargeting not proposed: this show's CIRQLIN page isn't published. It's proposed at the first read after it is."
    • Any other failure: "Retargeting not proposed: the show's links couldn't be read from CIRQLIN this run. The next run tries again."
  • No CIRQLIN tag: a show without a crm_base_tag keeps RT2's two links, the event's page and its ticket URL, named the same way.
RT14 · Several windows per tier

Each pixel and engagement source is built at several windows, each one its own audience, in place of one window per tier.

TierSource · windows
BottomTicket-link visitors · 7, 14 and 30 days
Checkout starters · 7, 14 and 30 days
Registrations · 30, 90 and 180 days (RT15)
Signups lists, no window
Mid BottomPage visitors · 14, 30 and 60 days
Checkout starters · 60 days (its first 30 days are Bottom's)
Similar lists, no window
MidVideo viewers 75 % · 90, 180 and 365 days, one set per page
Page and Instagram engagers · 30, 90 and 180 days
  • The longest window of each source is RT2's, so who is in each tier doesn't change, registrations aside. A tier reaches everyone in its longest windows, so the shorter windows add no one. They're their own audiences so Ads Manager shows how recent each tier's people are.
  • Leave-outs take every window of the hotter tiers (RT2 stands).
  • Names in Ads Manager: "{code} · {source} · {n} days", for example "XMPL-WHSE · Ticket-link visitors · 7 days". A per-page video set adds the page: "{code} · Video viewers 75 % · {page} · 90 days".
  • In the tier row, sources with the same windows share a line, and the windows read as words: "Ticket-link visitors · checkout starters · 7, 14 and 30 days". At 393 the same lines join with " · ".
  • Under test: this is layout A in RT17's test.
RT15 · Registrations in Bottom
  • Bottom gains registrations: the pixel's CompleteRegistration on the show's landing pages (one rule per landing_url, RT13), at 30, 90 and 180 days, beside the hand-uploaded Signups lists (Matas). The two overlap, and that's fine: the list also holds people the pixel never saw.
  • Row: "Registrations · 30, 90 and 180 days".
  • No registrations reaching the pixel from any landing page in 28 days: a line in brick, "No registrations from {landing pages} reach the pixel in 28 days, so Bottom counts signups from the signup list only."
  • RT8's signup line becomes "No signup list yet, so Bottom runs on {sources} only.", where {sources} lists what Bottom has: ticket-link visitors, checkout starters, registrations.
RT16 · Every active show
  • Matas wants it on every active show once built. Active means on sale, show day today or later, not cancelled, and able to take the campaign (RT8 and RT9's conditions).
  • The first 08:00 read after the gates are on proposes a Create line on every active show. The Retargeting panel is Live on every active show without arming each event. The rest of the refresh keeps R4's per-event arm.
  • Nothing is built until Matas accepts and applies each Create line.
  • A show already in the run-in starts at its stretch's share (RT11). RT9's line for legacy retargeting ad sets stands.
RT17 · Test both layouts

Matas, 17 Nov: test both layouts and keep whichever performs best. Each show runs two arms in a Meta A/B test, and the call is made once, across every show in the test.

ArmTiers
A · tiers by actionRT14 as written. Bottom = ticket-link visitors, checkout starters, registrations and Signups lists. Mid Bottom = page visitors, checkout starters 60 days and Similar lists. Mid = video viewers and engagers. The windows are labels.
B · by recency (J2's layout)Every source in every tier: ticket-link visitors, page visitors, checkout starters, registrations, video viewers 75 %, and page and Instagram engagers.
  • Bottom: every source at 30 days, plus A's high-intent actions at A's windows (registrations to 180 days) and the Signups lists.
  • Mid Bottom: every source at 60 days, plus the Similar lists.
  • Mid: every source at 180 days.
  • Shared rules. In both arms each tier leaves out the hotter tiers and buyers (RT2, RT12). RT13's links, RT1's settings and the reused ads are the same in both, so only the layout differs.
  • Two campaigns per show: {code} · Retargeting · A/B · A and {code} · Retargeting · A/B · B, with ad sets {code} · Retargeting · A/B · {A | B} · {tier}.
    • One Meta A/B test (an ad study) per show, on the show's ad account, holds the two campaigns as its two cells at 50 % each. Meta splits people at random, so nobody is in both arms.
    • The test runs from Apply to 23:59 London on the show day, unless the call ends it first.
    • RT9 counts these campaigns as the show's Retargeting campaign.
  • Money. Each arm gets half of the show's total (RT11), split 50 / 25 / 25 inside the arm, with RT4's £1.00 floor per tier.
    • A show whose total is under £6.00 a day can't fund both arms. It isn't entered: it runs one {code} · Retargeting campaign on layout A until the call, then on the winner.
    • The hold against the plan is the show's total, as before.
  • Inside each arm, separately (Matas): RT11's steps, and RT5's moves, holds and cuts.
    • A step raises each arm by half the rise.
    • A read offers at most one Move or Cut per arm.
    • Money never moves between arms.
  • Who's in the test: every active show (RT16) whose Create line is applied from the first read after the gates are on, until the call.
  • The call. It's on cost per attributed purchase, pooled across every show in the test: each arm's spend ÷ its attributed purchases, summed across shows, never averaged show by show. It's called only when all of these hold:
    • 14 days since the first show's test began;
    • at least 30 attributed purchases in each arm;
    • at least 3 shows in the test;
    • the cheaper arm is at least 15 % cheaper (CT7's margin).
  • The line. The call is one line for Matas, on the Retargeting panel of every show in the test. Accepting it once applies to all:
    • the losing arm's campaigns pause on every show;
    • each show's Meta A/B test ends;
    • each winning arm takes its show's whole total, split by budget up to room as a step is (RT11), with what no tier can take going back to the plan;
    • every new show starts on the winner as one {code} · Retargeting campaign.
    Rejecting leaves both arms running, and the line comes back at the next read.
  • Too close. When the margin is under 15 %, there's no call. From 28 days, still under 15 %: "No clear winner after 28 days: A £{a}, B £{b} per attributed purchase." with Accept and Reject, where accepting keeps A, the simpler layout.
  • Words.
    • Panel header: "{code} · Retargeting · A/B test". Under it, RT11's total line, then "£{half} a day to each arm · Meta splits people at random, so nobody sees both".
    • Arm headings: "A · tiers by action" and "B · by recency".
    • Arm B's audiences: "Every source · {30 | 60 | 180} days", with Bottom's second line "Registrations · 180 days · signup list".
    • Pooled block, headed "The test, every show": columns Arm, Shows, Days, Spend, Attributed purchases, Cost per attributed purchase.
    • Before the minimum: "Not called yet: day {d} of at least 14 · A {pa} and B {pb} attributed purchases, at least 30 each · {n} shows, at least 3".
    • Under the margin: "Too close to call: A £{a}, B £{b} per attributed purchase, {p} % apart (15 % needed)."
    • The call: Call · "Layout {X} wins the retargeting test" · "£{x} per attributed purchase ({nx}) against {Y}'s £{y} ({ny}) · {p} % cheaper · {n} shows · {d} days, {Ddd d} – {Ddd d Mon}" · "On accept: {Y} pauses on {n} shows and each show's {X} takes its whole retargeting budget. New shows start on {X}." · "Ends the Meta A/B test on {n} shows · pauses {n} campaigns · ad-set budget writes".
    • A show not entered: "Too small to test: £{total} a day is under £6.00, so it runs layout A only." on its Create line.
    • Phone: each arm is one card, headed by the arm and its budget, then "£{c} per attributed purchase · {n}", then one line per tier: "{tier} · £{b} a day · £{c} · {n} purchases · frequency {f}". The call reads "Layout {X} wins · £{x} vs £{y} · {p} % cheaper", then "{n} shows · {d} days", then Accept and Reject.
