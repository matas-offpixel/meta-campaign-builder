One Retargeting campaign per show, in three tiers

From the audit in #1298 (docs/session-logs/retargeting-funnel-audit-2026-11-17.md, §1–§4) and Matas's budget rule of 17 Nov. Each show on sale gets its own {code} · Retargeting campaign, separate from its on-sale campaigns, with budgets set on the ad sets. It has three tiers that never share a person: Bottom, Mid Bottom and Mid. Budget moves between them on cost per attributed purchase, each move a line to accept. The campaign runs to the end of the show day.

This record replaces the on-sale refresh's per-campaign Retargeting standing default ("This campaign has no retargeting ad set.") for every show that can take this campaign (RT9). R1–R6, C2–C9, E1–E4 and T1–T6 stand. The example figures are invented.

Revised 17 Nov, before merge, from #1300's review: buyers come only from a hand-uploaded Buyers list for now (RT2, RT8), the pool before create reads "Known after create" (RT3), and RB is redrawn.

Open, for Matas:
  1. Step heights. How far the budget rises in the payday stretch, the final month, the final two weeks and the final week (RT6). Until he sets them, the step lines are drawn with a slot, and the build ships with steps switched off.
  2. Signup lists in Mid Bottom. Whether the show's signup list also goes in Mid Bottom (RT2). Until he rules, it's in Bottom only.
  3. Buyers from a ticketing connection. Uploading a show's buyers from its ticketing connection means processing fan data, so it waits for him (RT2). Until then, buyers come only from a list uploaded by hand.

Rulings

#Ruling
RT1 · The campaign
  • One per show: {code} · Retargeting, objective Sales, with a daily budget on each ad set and none on the campaign. Its three ad sets are {code} · Retargeting · Bottom, · Mid Bottom and · Mid (the TT1 naming; J2's Ads Manager names in audit §1.1 aren't copied). The panel calls them Bottom, Mid Bottom and Mid.
  • What it optimises on is what the show's Purchase campaign would, with no 15-purchase trigger, because retargeting runs from the start of the on-sale:
    • the show's own-domain custom conversion when its own domain has had a purchase in 28 days (E1, T5);
    • otherwise the resolved pixel's standard Purchase on the platform path, when T2's guard passes (the show's platform carries at least half the pixel's purchases).
  • When it's proposed: from the show's first on-sale date (presale when stored, else general sale), as a Create line at the top of the panel (frame RB). Accept, then Apply accepted, as with every refresh line.
  • Apply takes the Purchase campaign's create path (audit §4.3), with each step idempotent: the campaign paused, then the three audience sets (RT2), then the three ad sets paused, then the ads, then everything switched on.
    • The ads reuse the creatives live in the show's on-sale campaigns, with the same ads in every tier.
    • Every tier shares these settings (audit §3.1): 7-day click + 1-day view, Advantage+ audience off, audience expansion off, Facebook Feed plus Instagram Feed, Stories and Reels, and the location of the show's on-sale ad sets.
  • Gates: the Purchase create's gates (campaign create, apply, the event armed Live) plus the audience-write gate. In Shadow the lines show and Accept is disabled, as on the refresh panel.
  • End: 23:59 London on the show day. It keeps running through show week (RT7).
RT2 · The tiers and their audiences

Every person is in one tier only. Each tier leaves out every audience the hotter tiers include, plus buyers. All three are built from one source set per show (the same pages, Instagram accounts, videos and URLs), so the windows can't drift apart as J2 Melodic's did (audit §3.3, point 4).

Because Mid Bottom leaves out Bottom, it holds page visitors who didn't reach the ticket link in 30 days and checkout starters from 31–60 days ago.
  • Lists are the show's customer lists with a role: Signups, Similar or Buyers. The customer-audience wizard on the client page gains a role and a show, and writes the list's row (today it writes none, audit §4.1). Lists have no window.
  • Buyers come only from a list uploaded by hand with the Buyers role, for this show. Nothing fetches or uploads buyers from a ticketing connection until Matas rules on it (open item 3); a later record adds that. Without a Buyers list, see RT8.
  • Lineup pages go in only where the operator's Facebook user holds a role on the page (at least ADVERTISE). Missing pages: RT8.
  • Signup lists in Mid Bottom: open for Matas. Until he rules, they're in Bottom only.
RT3 · The tier rows

The panel is headed "{code} · Retargeting". Under the title:

  • "£{total} a day · {pct} % of current daily spend";
  • "Running since {Ddd d Mon} · ends {Ddd d Mon}, 23:59";
  • the signal, in T4's words: on the platform path, "Attributed purchases on the {pixel name} pixel, shared with its other shows' sales"; with an own domain, "Attributed purchases on {domain}".

One row per tier, hottest first. At 1280 the columns are:

  • Tier: Bottom, Mid Bottom or Mid.
  • Audiences: the includes in short, for example "Ticket-link visitors · checkout starters · 30 days" on one line and "Signup list · 2 similar lists" on the next. A missing source shows as its RT8 line under them.
  • Pool: Meta's estimate for the ad set's targeting with its exclusions applied (the delivery estimate), in Meta's band: "3,900–4,600", or "Below 1,000". Before Apply the tier's audiences don't exist, so Meta has no estimate, and the cell reads "Known after create" (on a phone, "Pool known after create"). It never reads "—" or a figure from anything else.
  • Frequency: the ad set's 7-day frequency to one decimal, "4.3". A tier at 4.0 or more reads it in brick.
  • Leaves out: "Buyers", "Bottom · buyers" or "Mid Bottom · Bottom · buyers". Without a Buyers list the word "buyers" goes, and RT8's line explains why. With nothing left out, Bottom reads "—".
  • Budget: "£12.00 a day".
  • Cost per attributed purchase: 7 days, "£5.15", with the count, "16", in its own column. Spend with no purchase reads "No purchase yet". Before the campaign runs, the figure columns read "—".

A total row gives the campaign's spend, purchases and cost per attributed purchase for the 7 days. On a phone each tier is a card (RT10).

RT4 · The starting budget
  • Current daily spend is TF1's: the show's average Meta spend per day over the last 7 days, across its on-sale campaigns. The Tests and Retargeting campaigns aren't counted.
  • The total starts at 10 % of that, rounded down to the penny. The Create line offers 10 % · 15 % · 20 %, with 10 % chosen, because tier pools are smallest at the start (Matas: "10–20%").
  • The split is Bottom 50 %, Mid Bottom 25 %, Mid 25 %, to the penny, with any odd penny going to Bottom.
  • The floor is £1.00 a day per tier, or Meta's min_daily_budget when that's higher. A tier under the floor is lifted to it and the difference comes out of Bottom, so the smallest total is £3.00. At the floor, the line adds " · £1 a tier minimum".
  • No spend in 7 days: no budget can be set, and the line reads "No spend in the last 7 days to size a budget from."
  • Money: the total × the days to the show (today and the show day included) is held against the plan, from headroom or a move out of the main campaign, inside R5's 20 % cap, as the Purchase create does.
  • The Create line: "£{total} a day · {pct} % of current daily spend · Bottom £{b} · Mid Bottom £{mb} · Mid £{m}", and under it "£{hold} to the show ({n} days) · {from}".
RT5 · Moves, holds and cuts
  • The read: every morning at 08:00 London (TF2's time), over the last 7 days. Unanswered lines expire at the next read. A read offers at most one Move or Cut.
  • The measure is cost per attributed purchase only, never checkouts, landing-page views or registrations. A tier with spend and no purchase counts as dearest once its spend passes the cheapest tier's cost per attributed purchase.
  • Frequency is how a pool's size shows up. The same budget on a smaller pool raises it. A tier's room is the daily budget that would bring its 7-day frequency to 4: its average daily spend × 4 ÷ its frequency. A tier at frequency 4 or more has no room.
  • Move:
    • The receiver is the cheapest tier with at least 3 attributed purchases in the 7 days and frequency under 4.
    • The giver is the dearest tier.
    • It moves only when the receiver costs no more than 0.75 × the giver (C9's 0.75, here tier against tier).
    • The amount is half the giver's average daily spend, rounded down to the penny. It's capped three ways: the giver keeps £1.00 a day, the receiver stays within its room, and R5's 20 % cap applies. The total doesn't change.
  • Hold: when the cheapest tier is at frequency 4 or more, it isn't fed. The read shows a note, with nothing to accept, and the next tier in line can receive instead.
  • Cut: at frequency 6 or more, the tier's budget is cut to the budget × 4 ÷ its frequency (rounded down to the penny, never under £1.00). What's freed goes to the cheapest tier that could receive. If none can, it goes back to the plan.
  • Too little to rank: with no tier at 3 attributed purchases, the read says "No move today: no tier has 3 attributed purchases in 7 days."
  • The words. Each line says why, using cost per attributed purchase, pool and frequency:
    • Move · "{giver} → {receiver}" · "£{c1} per attributed purchase ({n1}) to £{c2} ({n2}) · {receiver} frequency {f} on {pool} · £{x} a day" · "Ad-set budget write, both sides"
    • Hold · "{tier} held" · "Cheapest at £{c} per attributed purchase, but frequency {f} on {pool}. It gets no more until frequency falls under 4."
    • Cut · "{tier} → {receiver}" · "Frequency {f} on {pool} · £{a} → £{b} a day, back to frequency 4 · £{x} a day to {receiver} (£{c} per attributed purchase, frequency {f2})" · "Ad-set budget write, both sides". With no receiver, the end reads "£{x} a day back to the plan".
  • Phone: Move "£{c1} → £{c2} per attributed purchase · £{x} a day"; Hold "{tier} held · frequency {f}"; Cut "Frequency {f} · £{a} → £{b} a day".
RT6 · Step-ups at payday and in the run-in
  • The payday stretch is the code's payday window (isInPaydayWindow): 18 days from the Thursday before payday. Payday is the last Friday of the month (lib/creative-lab/payday.ts), moved by A8 around bank holidays and Christmas.
  • The run-in has three steps, counted with daysUntilEvent: the final month from 30 days out, the final two weeks from 14, and the final week from 7. Each one replaces the last. None of them steps down before the show.
  • How high each step goes is open for Matas. Until he sets it, the step lines are drawn with a slot and steps stay switched off.
  • Overlaps don't compound. Where the payday stretch and a run-in step overlap, the higher of the two applies.
  • Split by room. A step raises each tier in proportion to its budget, but only up to its room. A tier at frequency 4 or more doesn't rise, and money no tier can take isn't raised.
  • Money (Matas, 17 Nov): first plan headroom, then a move out of the show's on-sale campaigns, inside R5's 20 % cap. Each step is a line to accept and names its source.
  • The write is a Meta budget schedule on each tier ad set for the step's dates, so Meta returns to the base budget by itself when a payday stretch ends. The build probes the budget-schedule endpoint read-only before writing one.
  • The line: Step up · "{Payday · Final month · Final two weeks · Final week} · {Ddd d Mon} – {Ddd d Mon}" · "£{total} a day × {step} · tiers rise by room, {tier} doesn't (frequency {f})" · "{from plan headroom | £{x} a day from {campaign}}". The panel's footer shows the next step, for example "Next step: payday from Thu 26 Nov".
RT7 · Show week
  • The on-sale refresh stops from show week's Monday. The Retargeting panel doesn't: its 08:00 read, its moves, holds and cuts, and the final-week step carry on until the show day.
  • J2's best week was show week, with 35 % of the tiers' purchases (audit §1.4). The operator doubled two tiers on the Wednesday, which is the move the refresh couldn't make.
RT8 · Empty and missing states

None of these blocks Create. Each one is a line under the tier it affects, in brick, with its action.

  • No signup list yet: "No signup list yet, so Bottom runs on ticket-link visitors and checkout starters only." Action: "Upload the signup list" (the client page's wizard, role Signups). No similar list adds no line.
  • A lineup page with no role: "{page} isn't in Mid: your Facebook user has no role on the page. Granting ADVERTISE on it adds it at the next read." On a phone: "{page}: no page role, not in Mid." Several pages give one line naming them all. With no usable page and no video views, Mid can't be built: "Mid has no audience yet: no page with a role and no video views." Its 25 % then goes to Bottom and Mid Bottom, 2 to 1.
  • No Buyers list: "No buyers list yet, so buyers aren't left out and will keep seeing these ads." Action: "Upload a buyers list", which opens the client page's customer-audience wizard with the role Buyers and this show chosen. The line is the same whether or not the client has a ticketing connection, because a connection doesn't supply buyers yet. There's no "Connect ticketing" action until Matas rules on open item 3.
    • With a list uploaded by hand: "Buyers list uploaded {d Mon} · {n} days old".
    • From 7 days old: "… · {n} days old: upload it again so new buyers drop out". Melodic's list was ten weeks stale (audit §3.3, point 5).
  • Not proposed. Each of these replaces the Create line:
    • "Retargeting starts when the show goes on sale ({Ddd d Mon})."
    • "No Retargeting campaign: these ads have no pixel to optimise on."
    • "No Retargeting campaign: this pixel mostly sells on {host} ({pct} % of {n} purchases in 28 days), not on {platform}." (T2's guard)
    • "Retargeting not proposed: purchases couldn't be read this run. The next run tries again." (T6)
RT9 · No more "Retargeting missing" on the show's other campaigns
  • The check moves from campaign to show. The refresh's Retargeting standing default ("This campaign has no retargeting ad set.", rules.ts:553) is no longer proposed on any of the show's campaigns when any of these holds:
    • the show has a {code} · Retargeting campaign, whatever its status;
    • its Create line is proposed (open, accepted or rejected);
    • it's in the "couldn't be read" state.
  • The on-sale panel shows one note in place of those lines: "Retargeting runs in its own campaign, {code} · Retargeting." (frame RD).
  • When the campaign is off: one line on the Retargeting panel, "{code} · Retargeting is off.", never a line per campaign.
  • The standing default stays as today only for shows that can't take this campaign: no pixel, or a pixel that mostly sells elsewhere. Before the on-sale date, nothing changes.
  • Legacy ad sets: a delivering ad set with "retarget" in its name in any of the show's campaigns doesn't stop the Create line. The line says "{n} retargeting ad set(s) already run in {campaign}: pause them once this is live." Nothing pauses them automatically, because drops skip "retarget" (rules.ts:571).
  • The Purchase campaign's retargeting pick (purchase-campaign.ts:370) never copies from {code} · Retargeting, and P4's "Copy into Purchase" lines skip its ad sets. Its tiers stay in their own campaign.
