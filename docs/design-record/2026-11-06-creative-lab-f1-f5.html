Creative Lab, redrawn — thumbnails and previews first, fields behind Edit

This redraws the Creative Lab section on an event's Overview tab, using Puzzle 4th Birthday Part 1 (27 Dec, Circuit) as the example. Drawn on Off Pixel's own ground — sand #F0C9A8, card #e8d4c0, ink #1e1810, system type, 1280 max width — with the CIRQLIN rules: numbers are the hierarchy, empties are honest, dashed is a modifier, our words get translated and their data never does. The olive / ochre / brick hexes here are close approximations; the build uses the app's existing semantic tokens. The artists, handles and copy are the event's. Every number, Spotify detail, like count and cost per result is a placeholder. Thumbnails are colour blocks standing in for the first frame of each clip.

THE CHANGES
  1. One strip says where you are. Four steps — Lineup, Clips, Captions, Round — each with a count and a state. The step that needs you is highlighted, and one sentence below names the next action. A what's working line sits under it once there's round data.
  2. Artists are entered once, by @-tagging. Type @Enz and pick from the Artists library or a Spotify match. The row carries a photo, Instagram/TikTok handles, Spotify ID and the Page. ★ = headliner, drag = draw order. The lineup is these rows; no separate lineup, headliner or name field remains.
  3. Spotify context on hover or expand: top 3 tracks, popularity, genres.
  4. Rights are one tick: ✓ Cleared · Matas, 6 Nov, recorded automatically, with an optional note. No rights-reference field.
  5. Clips are a grid in three groups — Keeping (with cost per result and verdict), New, Can't run — plus Find clips on Instagram, which ranks the artist's recent reels, shows likes and comments, and says why any were skipped.
  6. Captions show as the ad. Labelled generator controls, the caption rendered in a feed post with its clip, ✓ / ✕ / edit, and the verdict and cost on the ones that ran. A failed check says what to fix.
  7. The round shows what ships: a clips × captions grid, the count of ads, and why the button is off when it is.
  8. Ticketing and Tickets sold move out of Creative Lab — connecting ticketing goes to the client's Ticketing tab, and tickets to date goes to Reporting.

F1 · The next-step strip and what's working

It replaces Next payday Friday, 27 November 2026. 51 days to the event. — the countdown moves into the event header, where the date already is. Each step is a button that scrolls to its section. Counts are numbers first; the state word sits under them.

Before the first round has data, what's working isn't drawn at all — not a 0% or an empty sentence. It reads the client's own past rounds first (on Puzzle), then the event's own once round 1 has a verdict.

F2 · Lineup — entered once, by @-tag

The rows are the lineup. Drag ⋮⋮ to set draw order; ★ marks a headliner. The rights tick is the only rights control. Each row has one action; everything else is behind ⋯ Edit. Adding an artist is the @ field at the foot.

Mark cleared opens a small sheet: Cleared by Matas, today (filled in, not editable), an optional note, and Clear. The row then reads ✓ Cleared · Matas, 6 Nov with the note under it in muted text. To undo, use ⋯ → Remove clearance; it keeps the history. A clip from an artist who isn't cleared can sit in the grid, but it can't go into a round.

F3 · Clips — a grid, three groups, and Find clips on Instagram

F3–F5 show the same Lab after a first round has run, so the Keeping group, the verdicts and round 2 have something to draw; F1 is the event as it stands today. Every tile is the clip's first frame, with source, length, artist and one status. Keeping is what's running and earning its place: cost per result in numbers, plus the round's verdict. New is untested. Can't run is drawn dashed in brick with the reason in words. The rights text appears on hover, never on the tile.

Verdict chips: Winner filled olive · Loser filled brick · Holding outlined ink (ran, inside the band) · Untested dashed. Colour judges a clip only against the client's own band. A clip with no round data shows no cost — —, never £0.00. The Instagram pass drops reels with text over the video (they read as someone else's ad) and says so on the tile instead of hiding them. A reel whose file can't be fetched offers request from team, which drafts the ask.

F4 · Captions — shown as the ad

The generator is one row of labelled choices. Each caption renders in a feed post, with its real line breaks and the clip it would pair with. The lineup · proposed · long · passed line is gone: a passed check draws nothing, and a failed one says what to fix. ✓ approves, ✕ rejects, ✎ edits the copy in place.

The CTA label follows the event's stage, as the comms rules do: Sign up while collecting, Get tickets on sale. How many and Stage sit behind More at 393; the defaults are the event's stage and 3.

F5 · The round — what would ship

Approved captions across, usable clips down; each cell is one ad. Cells start on and can be tapped off. The total is the hierarchy. A clip that can't run never appears. While the round can't be proposed, the button says why in words.

Proposing a round keeps today's step as it is (the operator approves before anything launches). Once a round runs, its cells pick up the same verdict chips as the clips and captions, and those verdicts feed what's working in F1.

Data this needs

Artists library (org-wide): name, photo, instagram_handle, tiktok_handle, spotify_id, page_id. The lineup becomes event ↔ artist rows with position and headliner. Clearance per event artist: cleared_by, cleared_at, note. Clip candidates: reel id, like_count, comment_count, has_text_overlay, file_available, skipped_reason. Verdicts per clip and per caption per round: cost per result, results, verdict (winner · loser · holding · untested) against the client's band. Spotify context is read live and cached; it isn't stored as fact.
