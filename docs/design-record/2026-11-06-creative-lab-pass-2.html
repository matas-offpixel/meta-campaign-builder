Played-before comes from your own events, so it stays and leads, because it's the part we actually own. The popularity bar and the track list are struck, not left as empty boxes.

B · The trajectory, in build order

B1 · Rights rule change — clearance only gates the artist's own channels

New rule: any artist's clips can run from the promoter's Page. Clearance only unlocks the artist's channels (their Page or Instagram as the ad identity, and captions written in the artist's voice). So Can't run no longer means "not cleared". Luke Dean's clip moves to New, and the held items are the artist-voice captions. The chip's two states say what each one allows, not who's missing.

Clear for artist channels opens the same sheet as before: who and when are filled in automatically, plus an optional note. The chip is neutral (ink outline) when it reads Promoter page only, because nothing is blocked. It only turns olive when it reads Cleared — artist channels too. Held captions stay in the list with an ochre Held chip and the one sentence that unlocks them. They never go into a round, and the round's count leaves them out.

B2 · Instagram import and quality (M4b)

Four moments: the candidates (already listed by rank), the 8-frame text check, the crop picker for wide clips, and the render queue that ends in a playable 9:16 preview. A refusal is always one sentence saying what to do.

Crop picker: the frame is the clip's widest moment, and the bright window is the 9:16 crop. Drag it sideways (that's the focus point; it's stored, never typed in) and the filmstrip shows the same window across six moments, so a head cut off at 0:12 is visible before the render. Native 9:16 clips skip this step. Too low quality is decided after the crop, by resolution, and the refusal says what fixes it. Text check: the 8 frames show on request, with the flagged ones outlined in brick, and Import anyway exists because the check can be wrong. Renders: one row per render with a progress bar and its place in the queue. When it's done, the 9:16 preview plays with the caption burned in, as it will run.

B3 · The learning layer (across clients, Matas only)

There are three pieces: proven lines in the caption writer, an insights panel (in the Lab and in the campaign drawer), and an automatic "since last round" view. Client names from other clients appear only to Matas; a client looking at their own event sees the client tier and nothing from anyone else.

B3a · Proven elsewhere — chips in the caption writer

A chip is the line itself, a 4-bar strength signal and won n of m, counted in events, not ads. A line only qualifies after winning at 2 or more events. In a generated caption, a proven line carries a small ◆ so you can see which part was reused. The 🔒 line naming clients shows only on Matas's account.

B3b · Insights — the Lab panel and the campaign drawer

Order of scope: this client, then the vertical, then everyone. The toggle defaults to the narrowest scope that has enough rounds, and the confidence line says how much it's standing on. Bars show cost per result, so the shorter cost wins: the bar length is the inverse of the cost and the number on the right is the cost. The winner is olive; everything else is ink. No clear winner is a real result, drawn as near-equal bars with a dashed noise band and those words, never an empty chart. Meta spent on X of Y is one square per ad, filled if the ad got real spend.

B3c · Since last round — what changed, without a log

This is written from round verdicts, never typed. At most five chips; the rest go behind +n. A falling cost line is olive, a rising one brick.

B4 · Rounds over time — keep, kill, new

Each round is a column. A clip or caption that's kept carries straight across; one that's killed ends struck through with its verdict; a new one enters dashed. Round 2's plan is written from round 1's verdicts: keep the winners, put new captions on the winning clips, and pair new clips with the winning captions.

B5 · The asset bank (M6) — one board per event

Rows are asset types and columns are formats. Every cell is one of three things: a thumbnail (exists), a thumbnail with a ✓ (approved), or a dashed + (missing, with what's needed to make it). A cell that's held for approval is outlined in ochre. The count at the top is what you'll look at: how much is ready for the next push.

Every missing cell says what's needed (+ needs Reeshy's press shot, + from the designer's PSD), so the board doubles as the to-do list. An artist card's optional 15–20 s audio shows as a badge on the video cell. Artist-voice cells follow B1 and are held until that artist is cleared for their own channels.

B5 · The payday calendar

B5 · Ticket-warning approval — grounded in ticket data, Matas approves

Every warning shows the ticket number that makes it true, where it came from and when it was read. A warning the data doesn't support yet is locked, says how far off it is, and has no Approve button. This is the no-fabricated-scarcity rule, drawn. Sold out unlocks only at capacity, Final 100 only once 100 or fewer remain, and Price rise only when there's a dated tier change. Approve → Scheduled (around the next payday window) → Live; nothing goes live without Matas pressing Approve.

B6 · TikTok Spark posts — boosted as they are

A Spark post is the artist's own TikTok, boosted using a code from their team. It has no caption overlay and no crop, so it can't be paired with a caption. It sits in the clip grid with a TikTok badge and its code state, and in the round as its own row with a single boosted as is cell.

Adding a Spark post means pasting the code; the tile fills itself in from the post (thumbnail, author, opening words of the caption, how long the code is valid). An expired code shows the reason and who to ask, and its row drops out of the round count. The code itself is the artist's permission, but only for that one post. It doesn't clear the artist for anything else, so their artist-voice captions stay held (B1) until they're cleared.

C · Design rules

This is the full checklist that goes into every Cursor prompt. The file version, with the component table, is docs/creative-lab/design-rules.md.

  1. States before fields. Each section opens on what's true — a count, a chip, a preview — and the inputs that change it are behind `Edit`, `⋯` or a sheet. A section that opens on an empty form is wrong.
  2. Previews first. Anything that becomes an ad is shown as the ad: a clip as its first frame, a caption as a feed post, a render as a playable 9:16 preview, a warning as its artwork. Never a filename or a line of text standing in for it.
  3. At most 3 inputs visible per section before the user presses Edit or Add. A search or @ field counts as one input. Inputs are labelled; there are no unlabelled selects.
  4. One primary action per row or card. Everything else goes in `⋯`. The primary action is a verb on the thing (`Find clips`, `Approve`, `Use this crop`), never "Go" or "Submit".
  5. No raw IDs, hashes, file IDs, field names or reference strings on screen. No `events.status (…)`, no Drive IDs, no `proposed · long · passed`, no `/` line markers. Long provenance text (rights emails, notes) lives on hover or in Edit, never on the tile.
  6. Numbers carry a unit and a comparison. `£0.44 / signup`, `vs £0.58 in round 1`, `998 of 1,400 sold`. A number with no unit or no comparison isn't finished. Numbers are the biggest text in their cell.
  7. Errors are one plain sentence saying what to fix. "Needs a city this artist has played — add it to their row." Passed checks draw nothing. A refusal names the fix ("find a sharper copy or ask the team").
  8. Empty states say what unlocks them. "Approve at least 1 caption to propose round 1." Never "No rounds." and never an empty chart. "No clear winner" is a result and is drawn as one.
  9. Every component defines its 393 behaviour: what stacks, what scrolls sideways, what moves into a sheet, what drops to its own line. No horizontal page scroll; native selects are replaced by sheets.
  10. Colour tokens only. Sand, card, edit, ink, ink-2, ink-3, border; olive / ochre / brick to judge a number against the client's own band; platform tints to name a platform. No new hex values. Colour never carries meaning alone — a word or shape goes with it.
  11. Never fabricate scarcity. A ticket warning shows the ticket count that makes it true, its source and when it was read. If the data doesn't support it, it's locked.
  12. Cross-client data is Matas-only. Other clients' names, and anything drawn from their results, show only on his account (🔒 line). Clients see their own tier.
