Rulings

#Ruling
O63 Footage resolutionThis amends O42 and O62, for video only. Stills keep O19's "no upscaling".
  • It binds every format, 9:16 included, and is measured on the crop window: the window's width in source pixels against the output's 1080.
  • Up to 1.6×. A window at least 675 px wide may be scaled up to the output, with Lanczos. Under 675 px, that format isn't made: "Clip too small for {format} — {w} px wide in the crop, needs 675". The type is always set at the output's size, never scaled.
  • Why 1.6: Instagram's own clips usually arrive at 720 × 1280, which is 1.5× to 1080. That reads as normal phone footage. 2.7× visibly breaks up. 1.6 lets 718 to 720 px sources through with a little room; it is a limit, not a target.
  • Import takes the largest copy there is. Drive, press-kit and uploaded files are imported at their own size, never re-encoded smaller. An Instagram clip is its media URL's file. When one artist has clips both in 02 Source / Artist clips or Press kits and on Instagram, the folder clips are listed first in that artist's group. The Lab doesn't try to match duplicates.
  • For an artist whose footage is all 720p Instagram, that is enough: 720 × 1280 makes all three formats at 1.5×. For a clip too small for a format, the refusal line is the answer, plus asking the artist's team for the original. The Lab adds no new feature for that.
  • What it means today:
    • Luke 1 (956 × 718): 1:1 only (718 px, 1.50×). The 9:16 (404 px, 2.67×) and the 4:5 (574 px, 1.88×) aren't made.
    • Enzo 1 and Enzo 2 (720 × 1280): all three formats at 1.5×.
O64 The face floorThis amends O56.
  • The floor is a share of the output frame's width, so it reads the same whether a face is measured on the source or after scaling (44 / 720 = 66 / 1080 = 0.061 W).
  • The detector runs on the source frames, with no upscaled pixels, and its boxes are mapped into the output's geometry.
  • The floor rises from 0.06 W to 0.08 W (86 px at 1080; 58 px on a 720 source). Enzo 2's 0.061 W face is a booth or crowd face. A DJ in a medium shot is 0.12 W or more, and the artist card's face floor on stills is 216 px (0.20 W).
  • Faces under half the widest counted face's width still don't count (O19).
O65 The run refusalWhen the 0.5 s run limit refuses a clip and the share is within its 10 %: "Face in the type for {t} s at a time — choose another crop or clip", where t is the longest run to one decimal. When both fail, the share's line as now. The report gives both figures, for A and for B at every crop tried, so a refusal shows what was tried.
O66 The backing's shapeThis amends O58's padding and feather. The backing never has a hard edge and never sits under other type's ink.
  • Core (full opacity): line 1's ink box, padded 1 cap left and right. Above and below, the padding is the smaller of 0.5 cap and a quarter of the gap to the neighbouring type. The gap runs from line 1's ink to the next type's ink: line 2's cap top below, the date's or name's baseline above. With no type within 1.5 cap, the padding is 0.5 cap.
  • Feather (J2's fade, to 0): outward from the core. Vertically it runs over the smaller of 1 cap and what is left of the gap, so it reaches 0 exactly at the neighbouring type's ink. Horizontally it runs over 1 cap, ending at 0.07 / 0.93 W at the latest.
  • If the horizontal feather would be squeezed below 0.25 cap at 0.07 / 0.93 W, the horizontal padding shrinks first, but never below 0.25 cap.
  • The steps (40 / 60 / 80 %), the test on line 1's ink box only, and the entrance with the claim are unchanged.
  • The card in round 2: cap 48 px and gap 22 px give a padding of 5.5 px below, then a 16.5 px feather to 0 at line 2's cap top. Above, there is no type within 72 px, so 24 px of padding and a 48 px feather.

  • The backing cut hard at type and at 0.07–0.93 W: superseded by O66.
