Artwork overlay templates — T1 artist clip, T2 standalone message, T3 lineup artwork

This record's images live beside it in docs/design-record/2026-11-08-overlay-templates/ (30 files: the frames and the piece thumbnails). The design thread's channel can push text, not binaries, so that folder is committed by Matas from the attachment on the PR; until it lands the frames show their outlines on an empty plate. Three named templates for the assets the asset bank makes from a brand kit (pass 2 B5/B7, design-rules rule 14, asset bank issue 2 A5'). Each is drawn with Matas's real Puzzle assets — the designer's pixels, slots outlined — measured, and ruled as slots with sizes as a fraction of frame height. The 4:5 information rectangle is drawn on every frame. Every value not read from an asset or the PSD is listed under Invented.

O · Rulings

#Ruling
O1 One master, one rectangleEvery asset is composed at 9:16, 1080×1920. The 4:5 rectangle (1080×1350, y 285–1635, 14.84 % in from top and bottom) is the information rectangle: every slot sits inside it, so the 4:5 is a pure centre crop of the master. The 1:1 (1080×1080, 21.875 % in) is not a crop: it is the 4:5 arrangement scaled by 0.8 (1080 ÷ 1350) — every slot at the same fraction of the 1:1 rectangle, type at 0.8× its pixel size — with the background extended at the sides, rendered from the same props. Three files per asset: 9:16, 4:5, 1:1. Until H8 returns Bootzy, the 1:1 frames here show the file's crop; the built 1:1 is the 0.8× re-sit.
O2 Positions and sizesA slot's vertical position is a fraction of the target rectangle (4:5 or 1:1), so it lands the same way in each format. A slot's size is a cap height as a fraction of the 9:16 frame height; the pixel size is the same in 9:16 and 4:5, and 0.8× in 1:1. Widths are a maximum fraction of frame width. Text is centred unless the slot says otherwise. Nothing sits closer than 7 % W to a side edge. Every value is a ratio, rendered at the output size (1080×1920 from a 720×1280 UGC master, say); pixels are never copied between sizes.
O3 T1 artist clipFor artist content, still or clip. Slots: artist (one line; cap 0.1146 H — 220 px at 1080 — in the box 0.1750–0.2891 H; the size steps down at the face's own width until the name fits 80 % W, floor 6 % H; under the floor the name breaks first / last name on two lines at 6 % H; never condensed or scaled horizontally; centre 0.232 H = 12 % of the rect), details (four lines venue · town · date · time in the block 0.3036–0.4391 H, 0.328 W, centre 0.371 H = 32 % of the rect), claim (optional; one line, cap 0.039 H, at 0.7536–0.7927 H, centre 0.7732 H = 89 % of the rect, ≤ 0.80 W; a registered CTA piece lands where its canvas puts it, 0.72–0.81 H for the Locklead CTAs). Slack inside the rect: 0.027 H above the name, 0.059 H below the CTA. The middle 0.44–0.70 H carries no text: the face or the clip. In the 1:1 file the slots sit at 12 / 32 / 89 % of its height, type at 0.8× (the name's top is above the 1:1 line, so the re-sit stands). Between rungs only the CTA changes (FINAL 50 → FINAL 150): same cuts, same composition. These are the floating kit's slots (O14); a frame kit defines its own (O15).
O4 T2 standalone messageFor a message with no artist image. The floating kit's slots: claim (centred on the rect's centre = 50 % H, cap 6 % H, line pitch 8.75 % H, max width 86 % W; a number token — a count, %, or price — draws at 1.5× the line only when it is the message on its own line (£16.50; FINAL 100 alone); inside a sentence the number sits at the sentence size, as FINAL 50 2ND RELEASE TICKETS does; headline at most two lines plus one qualifier line at 0.6×; a CTA piece used here is placed by its ink box at the slot's cap height and steps down (O8) when that exceeds 86 % W), lineup or partners (top-right, right-aligned, cap 2.6 % H, pitch 3 % H, block top at 10 % of the rect = 21.9 % H, right edge 14.3 % W in; partners draw as logos at the same height when the event has them, else the typed lineup, max 6 lines), details (bottom-left, two lines date / venue, cap 2.66 % H, centre at 88 % of the rect = 76.7 % H, left edge 14.3 % W in), logo (bottom-right, the brand kit's wordmark piece, 15 % W wide, same centre line as details; + needs the piece until the kit has one). In a frame kit the corners are the frame (O15).
O5 T3 lineup artwork editThe PSD, unchanged except its live text layers, each a slot named by its layer name. Puzzle 4th Birthday has one: PART 1 ENZO SIRAGUSA LUKE DEAN… — one line "PART n" in the coral run, then one name per line, "&" before the last name in the coral run. Style runs are kept by role (first line, the "&" token), not by character index. The logo, the script and the venue · date · time lines are pixels inside 4th bday poster NEW: the kit lists them as locked · inside "4th bday poster NEW", and the date cannot change until the designer makes it a live layer — ask once, per kit. Hidden layers (uzzle-december-lineup-1080.jpg) are ignored. Rule 14 holds whole: shorten, never shrink; flagged if it can't be shortened.
O6 Type and edgeThe kit's faces — for the floating kit Bootzy Condensed TM Regular, all caps, white (255,255,255) — rendered on the font path, or the designer's own pixels when an artwork-face piece exists. No stroke on T1 or T2. The edge is the file's: a 1 px anti-aliased edge and a soft black drop shadow straight down — offset 0.007 W (8 px at 1080), blur ≈ 0.009 W (10 px), 25 % opacity. A registered piece carries it baked and is placed as-is; rendered text gets exactly this shadow, once. A frame kit carries whatever edge its references show (J2: the audit reports the scrim, O16, and no edge value on the type — owed with H8 (e)). T3 draws whatever effects the PSD's text layer carries (the audit reads them). The UGC caption's 2 px stroke (rule 13) is the TikTok Sans style only and never applies to an artwork overlay.
O7 Claim wordsA claim comes from the ladder (asset bank issue 2, A5'), lands in the claim slot in caps, names the release verbatim, and never carries a number the trigger didn't supply. Table below. "Live" and "selling fast" claims say now available / selling fast; a % claim says n% sold out; final X says FINAL X {release} TICKETS; sold out says {release} sold out; the roll-call (O12) says who sold out and what is left. Price is rarely the message: a price appears only on a price rung, and a number is enlarged only when it stands alone on its line (O4).
O8 Too longT1 and T2 slots step, never a third line. T1 claim: step 0 one line at 4 % H (≤ 36 characters); step 1 two lines at 4 % H; step 2 two lines at 3.4 % H (≤ 2 × 42). T2 claim: step 0 two lines at 6 % H (≤ 2 × 30); step 1 two lines at 5.2 % H (≤ 2 × 34). Past the last step the words are shortened in a fixed order (drop TICKETS → drop NOW → the release's number form, "3RD" for "3RD RELEASE" only when the release word is already on the frame) and the proposal says what was dropped; if it still doesn't fit, it's flagged and can't be approved (rule 14). The fit meter reads the step reached ("step 1 of 2 · 2 lines"). T3 never steps: rule 14 as written.
O9 Lineup and partnersT2's top-right slot draws the event's typed lineup (the Lab's artist rows, in their order; "& MORE" when the event says more TBA) or the event's partner logos when the kit has them. Never typed on the asset. T1's details read the event's venue, city, date and times. T3 reads the lineup from the same rows.
O10 One component per templateT1, T2 and T3 are three components in design-rules.md with the slots above. A new asset type (artist card, key-moment push) is a T1 or T2 with its claim set, not a fourth template, until a record says otherwise.
O11 Pieces and the font pathEvery T1 / T2 slot is filled one of two ways, in this order of preference. (a) A designer-exported piece from the event's piece library — three shelves, artist names · event info · call to action; names and info stay fixed for the campaign, the CTA shelf grows with the ladder and the clips — imported as exact and preferred when one exists for the slot and its face matches the artwork. (b) The font path: the kit's licensed faces on the Lambda with the measured edge (H8), the primary renderer for every slot, used whenever no matching piece exists. A slot with neither reads + needs the piece (rule 11); render with {face} is its secondary action only when the font is licensed and H8 has passed for that kit. Placement of a piece: by its ink box, scaled uniformly to the slot's cap height, never by its canvas, never stretched; a registered piece (its canvas is the 9:16 frame) is placed as-is because its ink box already sits at the slot. If the scaled piece exceeds the slot's width the slot's size steps apply (O8), then it is flagged. Styling is the designer's by construction on path (a) and the measured kit on path (b).
O12 The sold-out roll-call rung and kit redA two-line CTA. Line 1 in kit red: every release in state sold_out, in order, joined with commas and "&", ending SOLD OUT — "EARLY BIRD, 1ST, 2ND & 3RD RELEASE SOLD OUT". Line 2 in white: the live release's current claim — "FINAL RELEASE RUNNING LOW", "FINAL 100 FINAL RELEASE TICKETS". Trigger: two or more releases sold_out and one still on sale. The words are generated from event_releases, so the approval card shows them pre-written; only the piece needs making (a piece if the designer supplies one, else the font path). Preferred: whenever its trigger is met the roll-call is proposed ahead of a plain percentage — social proof is the point. In T1 it sits in the lower third, two lines of 0.039 H (the registered piece at 0.7052–0.7849 H; the red line's cap is 0.022 H on the piece); in T2 it is the central claim at the slot's cap height, stepping down to fit 86 % W. kit-red is the kit's second colour, measured #FE3232 (254, 50, 50) on the piece; the PSD's PART 1, "&" and "›" use it too. It is a kit colour (the artwork's), not a UI token: the UI never uses it for meaning (rule 10). Each kit names its own second colour.
O13 Face matchAt import, each piece's glyph aspect (ink width ÷ cap height per character) is measured against the kit's reference — the artwork's face, taken from a registered piece or the statics. The shelf shows the result as a chip: artwork face ✓ or other face. An other-face piece is never auto-picked for a slot; Matas can place it by hand and the proposal says so. For Locklead: the four condensed pieces match; the fifteen 4:5 pieces do not (IoU 0.20 at the statics' positions).

The piece library (O11), the face chips (O13) and the font path apply per kit: J2's two faces are identified and licensed by the same audit as Bootzy (H8, per kit). A new kind of kit is a new row here before any template is built from it.
