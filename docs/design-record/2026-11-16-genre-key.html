A genre key per event, and how genre-sourced learnings are worded

This record rules the design questions raised by docs/session-logs/genre-learnings-audit-2026-11-15.md, with the build decisions ops has already made:

  • a new column, events.genre_family, holding one value from a fixed list per event, confirmed by an operator;
  • genre learnings computed in shadow;
  • a genre pool switched on for a job only at 5 or more shows across 2 or more clients, once the real grouping beats 90 % or more of shuffles.

The audit found that today genre doesn't separate learnings from show-to-show noise. So the key is built now and kept out of every reader until a job passes. Every surface here is internal. Example counts and families are invented.

Rulings

#Ruling
GK1 · The field and its labels

The event form gets one field, "Genre". The operators already say "genre", and the free-text tags beside it are called genres, so it isn't renamed "sound". The choices, in this order, with their stored keys:

  • "No single genre" is a choice the operator makes, kept apart from "Not set" (nothing confirmed yet). Neither one joins any genre.
  • Inside a sentence, a label is written in lower case: "house / tech house shows".
  • The list is fixed. A new family is a new record, not a free entry.
GK2 · A suggestion, never pre-selected
  • The field starts at "Not set". Nothing is ever selected or saved without an operator.
  • When Off/Pixel can guess, from the event's genre tags or else its lineup, a line under the field reads "Suggested: {label}, from the genre tags" or "… from the lineup", with the action "Use it". Taking it is the operator's confirmation.
  • Why: a family pools a show into other clients' learnings once a job is on, and the audit's own map was a guess. A wrong genre spreads. A missing one only leaves a show out.
  • Backfill runs the same way: Matas confirms the events with learning facts one by one, from the suggestion, in one pass. Nothing is written in bulk.
  • The helper line under the field reads exactly: "One per show. Shows of the same genre can share learnings once there are enough of them."
GK3 · Shadow is internal and silent
  • While a job's genre pool is off, its genre rows are stored and checked, and no reader shows them. That covers the Lab writer, the insights rail, plan predictions and every client surface.
  • The one place shadow shows is an operator status line per job, on the learning-refresh report:
    • "Genre pool off · {job}: {n} {label} shows across {m} clients. Needs 5 shows across 2 clients, and to beat 90 % of shuffles. Today: {p} %."
    • When it switches on: "Genre pool on · {job} · {label}: {n} shows across {m} clients, beats {p} % of shuffles."
  • Shuffle percentages appear only in this status line, never in a learning.
GK4 · How a genre-sourced learning is worded

Wherever one appears once its pool is on, it says it isn't the client's own result:

  • The source names the genre, the shows and the clients, as counts: "From {n} {label} shows across {m} clients". For example: "From 9 house / tech house shows across 3 clients".
  • It never names another client or another client's show. D10's "never another client's shows" stands.
  • The client's own result always wins. Genre is a fallback. When it's used because the client's own is thin, the line says so: "Not enough of {client}'s own shows yet, so this is from {n} {label} shows across {m} clients."
  • Lab writer's shape line: the finding first, then the source line under it, as in frame G3.
  • Plan predictions (a comparable set when the venue has fewer than 2 shows): "Compared with {n} {label} shows across {m} clients".
  • Never on a client's share link or client dashboard. Genre learnings are operator tools, and an aggregate of other clients' shows doesn't belong on a client's page.
GK5 · The insights rail's scope
  • While every active client is music, the scope picker reads Client · Genre · All. "Vertical" comes back only when a non-music client does.
  • "Genre" is a choice only when the event has a confirmed family and that family's pool is on for the rail's job. Otherwise it's dimmed, with "Not enough {label} shows yet.", or "No genre set for this show." when the family isn't set.
GK6 · The reference library (D10)

D10 stands. Genre can filter a client's own library, for example Electric Brixton's garage nights. It never adds another client's shows.

GK7 · Few shows never read "strong"

At genre scope, a row needs at least 3 shows before it shows at all, as the build plans. Separately, at any scope, a row from fewer than 3 shows never reads "strong". Its confidence wording tops out at the level below. Whether client-scope rows also need 3 shows to show at all is a statistics call for the build, not a wording rule.

Acceptance

  • The event form at 1280 and 393: "Not set" by default, the suggestion line and "Use it", the helper line exactly, and the list in GK1's order, ending with "No single genre".
  • No path writes genre_family without an operator: no bulk write, no default.
  • While a pool is off, no reader returns a genre row. A test asserts it per reader.
  • Learning lines carry GK4's source wording. A test asserts that no other client's name appears in any genre-sourced line, and that none renders on a share link.

Invented here

  • The field name "Genre", the nine labels, their order, and "No single genre".
  • "Suggested: {label}, from the genre tags" / "… from the lineup", and "Use it".
  • "One per show. Shows of the same genre can share learnings once there are enough of them."
  • The two status lines in GK3.
  • "From {n} {label} shows across {m} clients", the fallback sentence, and "Compared with {n} {label} shows across {m} clients".
  • The rail's "Not enough {label} shows yet." and "No genre set for this show."
