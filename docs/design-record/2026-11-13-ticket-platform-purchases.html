Ticket-platform purchases: a shared signal, used honestly

This record amends E1–E3 of 2026-11-13-per-event-purchases.html (#1211). That record is merged and stays as it is; where the two differ, this one rules. E4 (the pixel) stands.

Matas, 13 Nov: for shows selling on ticket platforms (Skiddle, RA, See Tickets, Dice), a Purchase campaign may optimise on the pixel's purchases, shared across shows and not split per show.

Why it's needed. #1212's read-only dry run (HE1, 13 Nov, data to 12 Nov) found no show with a single purchase on its own domain, because buyers check out on Skiddle. The five NX shows and EBR26-KREAM have their own domains with 0 purchases. All six run on a pixel other than the stored one; Meta names the NX one "NX Live Skiddle". Under E1 alone, the Purchase campaign would almost never be proposed for these clients.

Rulings

#Ruling
T1 · Which shows take the platform path

A show takes the platform path when both of these hold:

  • Its ticket URL's host is a ticket platform. That means Skiddle, RA, See Tickets and Dice (Matas's four), plus Universe and Ingresse, which E1 already names as ticket sites. The list is a config that ops can extend.
  • No purchase has reached its own domain in 28 days, or it has no own domain at all.

This takes in three of E3's dead ends whenever the ticket URL is on a platform: "ticket site only", "nothing reaches" and "no domain of its own". A show with no ticket URL is unchanged.

T2 · The trigger, and what the campaign optimises on (a)

E1's principle still holds: the count that triggers is what the campaign would optimise on. On the platform path the campaign optimises on the resolved pixel's standard Purchase event. So the trigger is the purchases Meta credits to this show's ads, which is the signal Meta will learn from.

  • Trigger: 15 or more purchases attributed to this show's ads in 14 days, on the resolved pixel (E4).
  • Counted from the show's on-sale date. The count runs from the later of the window start and the show's first on-sale date (its presale when stored, else its general sale). A purchase credited before the show could be bought is certainly another show's: 22 of Julian Fijma's 24 landed before its presale. When the show has been on sale for less than 14 days, the sentence says "since {d Mon}" instead of "in 14 days".
  • Guard: the pixel must mostly sell where this show sells. In Meta's domain breakdown, the show's platform must carry at least half of the resolved pixel's purchases over 28 days. Otherwise optimising on the pixel's Purchase would chase another site's buyers. Example: Fever 105's ads run on the "Electric Studios Universe Pixel", whose purchases mostly land on universe.com, while Fever 105 sells on Skiddle. No proposal, and the panel says why (T4).
  • It optimises on the standard Purchase event, with no custom conversion. Once the guard passes, a custom conversion on the platform's domain would cover most of the pixel's purchases anyway. It would add a write and change nothing.
  • The pixel-wide host count is never the trigger. 473 purchases on skiddle.com in 14 days on the NX pixel says the pixel has signal. It doesn't say these ads earn any of it.
T3 · Cross-campaign moves (b)
  • Allowed between this show's own campaigns on cost per attributed purchase: the campaign's spend over the purchases credited to it since the on-sale date.
  • Both campaigns must optimise on the same resolved pixel. Each needs at least 5 attributed purchases in the week. C9's 1.5× / 0.75× thresholds apply.
  • Moves never cross shows. The budget is per event, and shared purchases make one show's cost per purchase another show's too.
  • Lines name the measure as it is: "cost per attributed purchase", never "cost per sale".
  • Under the bar, or when the guard fails, moves stay inside each campaign as before.
T4 · What the panel says (c)

The platform path has three states, with strings in F1. Each one says the signal is shared and never blocks: the shared signal is a fact, not a fault.

  • Proposed: 15 or more attributed, and the guard passes.
  • Under the bar: under 15, and the guard passes.
  • Pixel sells elsewhere: the guard fails, whatever the count.

The attributed line isn't repeated under these states, because there it is the count. In its place a context line gives the platform's share: "Skiddle took 81 % of this pixel's 836 purchases in 28 days." E3's "ticket site only" state is retired; a show selling only on a platform now lands here.

T5 · The own-domain rule first (d)
  • E1 still applies, and it applies first. A show whose own domain has had at least one purchase in 28 days (a CIRQLIN checkout or an embedded ticket widget) takes E1's own-domain count and its per-show custom conversion, which is more precise than the shared signal.
  • No such show exists today. #1212's read found 0 purchases on every own domain.
  • When an own domain has purchases but its ticket URL is also on a platform, E1 wins. The platform path is only for shows whose own domain sees nothing.
T6 · Re-rulings from #1212
  • 28 days, not 90. Meta's domain breakdown goes back only 28 days, so E2's test and F2's "nothing reaches" sentence say 28 days (Matas, 13 Nov).
  • The delivering window for E4 is the 14 days to the window end, the same span as the count.
  • The pixel's name is Meta's. The setup line uses the name Meta gives the pixel ("NX Live Skiddle"), not a CIRQLIN label. #1211's frames, which wrote "NX LOVES", are superseded on this point.
  • A ninth state: couldn't be read. A missing token, a Meta error or a CIRQLIN page that didn't load gives "Purchase campaign not proposed — this show's purchases couldn't be read this run. The next run tries again." At 393: "No Purchase campaign: purchases not read this run." It's never shown as a zero, and the raw reason goes to the evidence and the ops log, never the panel. The attributed line stays under it.
