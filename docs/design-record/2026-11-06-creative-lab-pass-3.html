Rule 13 · The locked caption style on every clip preview

Any caption on UGC uses the approved Luke Dean render (docs/creative-lab/ugc1-side-by-side.png): TikTok Sans, weight 400, width 120, white with a black stroke outside the letters, centred, sentence case, the block sitting about 36% of the way down. Play controls sit in the bottom corner, clear of the text. Every preview that shows a caption on a clip uses this one component: the feed post, render rows, round cells and the timeline.

Position, measured: in the approved render the caption block's centre sits 35.5% down the full 9:16 frame (35.4% in our render, 35.5% in the original UGC), and the block is about 43% of the frame wide. That's what's drawn here and what #1092 builds.

The four previews, redrawn

At round-cell and timeline size the caption won't be readable, and it doesn't need to be: what shows is where the text sits on which clip. Rule 14 · Artwork assets come from the artwork, never the caption style

Lineup overlays, artist cards, ticket warnings and key-moment pushes have to look like the rest of the campaign. So they're built from the designer's open artwork file (PSD) and the campaign's own font files, editing only the text layers the designer made. The locked caption style is for UGC captions only. If the artwork or a font is missing, the asset is missing; it's never rebuilt in a fallback style. B7 draws how that works.

B7 · Brand kit and artwork editing

One kit per event, or per campaign when several events share artwork. There are three moments: upload (the open artwork file and its fonts; layers detected), make a variant (pick an asset type; Claude proposes the text change and the ticket number behind it; preview in three formats beside the original), and approve (the same approval card as B5). The board from B5 gains a "from artwork" source for its artwork rows.

B7a · The kit — artwork, layers, fonts

Layers are detected, not configured. Every text layer becomes a slot, named from the layer name and checked by its content ("27.12" reads as a date), with its font and the most characters it can hold before it overflows the designer's box. Every image, shape and logo layer is locked. A text layer the designer didn't mean to change (the title, the venue) can be locked with one tap. A slot whose font isn't uploaded stays visible but faded, and the sentence names the exact font file to upload.

B7b · Make a variant — Claude proposes, you approve

Asset types that the ticket data doesn't support yet are drawn dashed and say when they unlock (80% sold · at 1,600), the same rule as B5's locked approval card. The proposal names which layer changes, the before → after copy, the fit meter for that slot, and the ticket number that makes it true. The previews are rendered from the PSD with the campaign's fonts, at 4:5, 9:16 and 1:1, beside the untouched original. 9:16 and 1:1 reflow the designer's layout only where the PSD has artboards for them. Without those artboards they show No 9:16 artboard in the file — ask the designer rather than a stretched guess.

B7c · When the copy doesn't fit the slot

Shorten, never shrink. The designer's type size and box are fixed. When copy is too long, Claude shortens it and says what it dropped and where that information still lives (ochre). If it can't be shortened without losing meaning (a lineup can't lose an artist on its own), it flags it in brick, with one action, and there's no Approve button until it fits. The fit meter is the same everywhere: a bar against the slot's limit, and the limit is a solid line.

B7d · On the board

The asset board from B5 gets a small from artwork badge on the lineup-edit, artist-card, ticket-warning and key-moment rows. When there's no kit, those rows' missing cells read + upload the artwork file instead of + make. Approving a B7 variant files it straight into its cell as ✓ and puts it on the payday calendar.
