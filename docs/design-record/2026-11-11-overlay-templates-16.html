Overlay templates — issue 16: Matas's answers, and the triple-fit

This issue covers two things. First, Matas has answered four open questions (O103–O107), and #1182's fixed centre crop needs a ruling. Second, it adds a new video layout. Matas, 11 Nov: "if we are short of footage and viral landscape footage is available on an artist's feed, then we can take that and triple fit it in a 9x16 edit, so each part takes 1/3 of the video, and only 1 single audio is played from one of the clips." He then corrected the first draft of this record: "it's 3 of the same clip using the same audio." So a triple-fit is one landscape clip shown three times. That is O108–O116. Issues 1–15 stand except where a row says otherwise.

The triple-fit

One landscape clip, stacked three times in one 9:16, each copy a third of the height, all in sync, with the clip's own sound played once. T1's type sits in issue 15's band. TF1 is the frame. TF2 is what Feed's centre 4:5 shows of it. TF3 is how the part is cut from its source. The three colours below mark the three copies of the same footage.

Rulings

#Ruling
O108 When it's offered
  • A triple-fit clip is one too small for its own 9:16 (its 9:16 window is under 675 px, O93) whose part window (O109) is at least 675 px wide. It's imported, not refused. Its tile has one status line:
    • "Landscape — triple-fit only".
  • These tiles sit in their owner's group (W2), in a "Triple-fit only · {n}" group after the clips that can run and before "Can't run". They have no selection tick, so they never go into a single-clip round.
  • A clip under both floors keeps "Too small for 9:16". O93's import refusal applies only when both floors fail, with its words unchanged.
  • The offer: "Make a triple-fit" shows on a triple-fit clip's tile when the owner is short of 9:16 footage, meaning they have fewer than 3 clips that can run in 9:16 (O93, O102: 9:16-capable and not refused). 3 is a round's default "How many". The count is of 9:16 clips, not parts. One triple-fit clip is enough to make one triple-fit.
  • Never automatic: the Lab never proposes a triple-fit, and a triple-fit never takes a 9:16 clip's place in a round. A landscape clip that does make its own 9:16 runs as itself, and is never offered as a triple-fit. A portrait clip is never a triple-fit.
  • Luke Dean (sizes from the 10 Nov size table):
    • Luke 15, 16, 18 and 21 are 1920 × 1080. Each 9:16 window is 607 px, under the floor. Each part window is 1822 px, so all four are triple-fit clips, at 0.59×.
    • Luke 1 is 956 × 718. Its 9:16 window is 404 px. Its part window is 956 px, so it is a triple-fit clip too, at 1.13×.
O109 The layout
  • One clip, three times. Parts 1, 2 and 3 are the same clip, through the same window, showing the same frame at the same timecode. They are in sync from first frame to last, never offset, so the one soundtrack matches all three pictures.
  • Each part is 1080 × 640: part 1 is y 0–640, part 2 is y 640–1280, part 3 is y 1280–1920. They are exact thirds, with no gaps and no dividers. The seam is where one copy's bottom edge meets the next copy's top.
  • Crop: one window for all three parts, never staggered. The window has the part's shape (1.6875 : 1) and is as wide as the source allows: width = min(source width, source height × 1.6875).
    • A 16:9 source keeps its full height, and the window is centred, with crop_focus_x moving it across the slack (1920 × 1080 → a 1822 × 1080 window).
    • A 4:3 source keeps its full width, and crop_focus_y places it (O41) (956 × 718 → 956 × 566).
    • Staggering the crop would buy almost nothing: a 1920 × 1080 source has 98 px of slack, and Luke 1 has 152 px.
    • The focus is set once on the clip and holds for the whole length.
  • Scale: O63's limit, at most 1.6× from the window, which is the 675 px floor. O69's filter applies.
  • The render copy for a triple-fit clip wider than 1080 is its part window at 1080 × 640 (Lanczos, CRF ≤ 16, as O68 and O101). The composition places it three times.
  • Length: the clip's stored window, at most 30 s. Under 6 s it isn't offered, because the claim needs 1.5 s plus at least 3 s on screen (O37).
  • Feed shows part 1's lower 355 px, all of part 2 and part 3's upper 355 px (TF2). Part 3 sits under the Reels controls. Every copy is the same footage, so nothing is lost that another part doesn't show.
O110 Sound
  • The clip's own track, once, from its in-point and in sync with all three pictures.
  • It is played once, never layered three times.
  • A clip flagged "Music match — mute or replace before launch" carries the flag onto its triple-fit, as on any clip.
  • A clip with no audio track runs silent, as it would on its own.
O111 Type
  • A triple-fit takes T1 on a clip, arrangement B only (O54, moved by O97):
    • the lockup at 353;
    • the name's cap top at 469;
    • the date above the claim;
    • the claim's last baseline on 1238.
    Everything stays inside issue 15's band.
  • A's date (632–689) would cross the seam at 640, so A is never used. No type ink sits within 24 px of a seam (y 616–664 and 1256–1304). B's top block ends at 603 and its date starts at 963.
  • The top block sits on part 1, and the date and claim on part 2. Part 3 carries no type.
  • O55's bands stay anchored to the blocks and run across the seams. O40's contrast is read on the pixels under each type box. Red is decided per triple-fit.
  • Not on a triple-fit: UGC captions and quote mode, because their blocks centre on 0.355 H and 0.28 H and cross the 640 seam. T2 isn't used either (O116).
O112 Faces
  • One footage window under two sets of type.
    • Part 1's lockup and name sit on part-local y 353–603.
    • Part 2's date and claim sit on part-local y 323–598 (963–1238 less 640).
  • So the face test reads the window against the union of the two, in the part's own coordinates:
    • the floor is 0.08 of the part's width (O64);
    • time in the type (O39, O65, O71) is measured against that union.
    Part 3 adds no boxes.
  • Crop candidates (O56) are tried once for the clip, because all three parts share the window.
  • A refusal uses O71's words unchanged.
O113 RightsOne clip, so its own rights decide the channels (rights_ref, and the "✓ Cleared — artist channels too" gate). A triple-fit runs wherever that clip could run. W4 holds.
O114 In the Lab
  • "Make a triple-fit" turns the clip into one 9:16 clip. It has its own tile in its owner's group, "Triple-fit · #{n}".
  • It is selectable, and it pairs only with T1, arrangement B.
  • It renders, launches and lists in "From the Lab" as one 9:16 asset (O95).
  • Its source clip stays as its own tile, still "Landscape — triple-fit only".
  • Deleting it is never needed. Unticking it takes it out of rounds. Making a second triple-fit from the same clip isn't offered.
O115 Invented hereThese values are invented, not measured, and are judged on drafts:
  • the 24 px seam clearance;
  • the 6 s minimum;
  • "fewer than 3" for the offer;
  • one shared window rather than staggered crops;
  • every new line of words in O108 and O114.
O116 The promoter's triple-fit
  • Promoter clips become triple-fit clips at import (O108), and their tiles show it.
  • "Make a triple-fit" isn't shown for the promoter yet. Promoter footage takes T2 on a clip, and T2's lineup runs from 397 to 698 (or from 353 when it moves up), which crosses the 640 seam.
  • T2 on a triple-fit needs its own layout in a later issue, with drafts.
