A · Rulings added in issue 4

#Ruling
A19 Rung 1 splitThe default ladder's first rung becomes two rungs.
  • Rung 1 — "{release} live". The move from presale into on sale (e.g. "GENERAL SALE LIVE"). Trigger: the release's list time has passed and its state is on sale. Unchanged from the old rung 1 except the words.
  • Rung 2 — "{release} selling fast" (new). The move between tiers, pushing the next one. Trigger: the release's state is selling fast (day 0 — no wait), or the release is on sale while every earlier release is sold out. Stored as state · selling_fast · {"days": 0, "or_earlier_sold_out": true} (the option's name is the builder's).
  • Matas ruled the second trigger: an earlier tier selling out is the evidence that this one is selling fast. With two or more earlier releases sold out, the roll-call (A14, A21) is preferred over rung 2 alone, as it is over a % rung.
  • The words are generated, never typed: {release} is the release's name as stored. On an asset the claim takes the kit's case (overlay O23 as amended): Puzzle's assets say "GENERAL SALE LIVE", "3RD RELEASE SELLING FAST". The approval card and the validator use the words as stored.
A20 The 90 % rung stays"Selling fast for N days, no count → {release} 90% sold out" (the client's choice of "final X {release} tickets" kept) stays exactly as it is, and moves from position 2 to position 3. Its days count from the release's selling-fast state_at, not from rung 2's proposal. Every later rung moves down one: the % rungs 4–8, final X 9, sold out 10, the roll-call 11, velocity 12, price rise 13, countdown + % 14, lineup addition 15. Fifteen rungs.
A21 The roll-call's line 2Line 2 is the live release's own highest met rung below sold out (A14). Because the roll-call's trigger (2+ sold out, one on sale) also meets rung 2's second trigger, that rung is never lower than "{live release} SELLING FAST" — so line 2 can no longer read "… live · selling fast" or "… live". Examples: "EARLY BIRD, 1ST & 2ND RELEASE SOLD OUT" / "3RD RELEASE SELLING FAST". A higher met rung replaces it: "FINAL 100 FINAL RELEASE TICKETS" when the final release is at final X, "FINAL RELEASE 90% SOLD OUT" after N days selling fast.
A22 The 17 clients' existing rung 1Rewritten in place, by one migration:
  • A client whose position-1 row equals the 211 seed exactly — trigger_kind, trigger_value, claim_template and options, compared byte for byte — gets "{release} live" as its rung 1, its rungs 2–14 moved down one, and the new rung 2 inserted.
  • A client whose rung 1 differs in any of those has edited it: the migration leaves that client's whole ladder untouched and names the client in its output. Matas decides there; "Reset to default" gives the new fifteen.
  • Rows are moved, never deleted, so every approved claim's link to the rung that allowed it survives. Today all 17 match the seed (data note).
  • The default for new clients and for "Reset to default" is the fifteen-rung ladder.
A23 A live claim ends when the next release goes live"{release} live" is the claim about a moment, the move into on sale. It is true for a release from its list time until a later release's list time passes; after that the later release is the one that is live, and an earlier release saying "live" would point buyers at the wrong tier. So at any time at most one release per event may propose rung 1: the latest whose list time has passed.
  • Puzzle 4th Birthday, today: Presale (21 Oct) and General sale (30 Oct) are both on sale. General sale is the later live release, so it proposes "GENERAL SALE LIVE". The presale's live claim ended at 30 Oct 12:00: it is not proposed. The ops proposal of both "PRESALE LIVE" and "GENERAL SALE LIVE" is not confirmed.
  • An earlier release still on sale can make any other claim its trigger allows (rung 2, the % rungs).
  • Timing is AB5's: "{release} live" runs at the release time. A late proposal (General sale's is eleven days after its list time) is still true and Matas may approve it; the card says when the release went live.
