Retargeting, re-ruled to the funnel

Matas ruled on 26 Nov: retargeting follows the approved funnel infographic (scratch/retargeting-funnel-reruling-2026-11-26.md, Request 1, with point g and his answers the same day). This is the record lib/retargeting/ is rebuilt against, and that M3c’s plan_retargeting and create_retargeting follow. The 17 Nov records aren’t edited.

  • Three tiers, Cold, Warm and Hot, one window per source. Each tier leaves out every tier to its right, and buyers.
  • Retargeting’s share of the show’s daily spend runs from 25 % at announce to 50 % in the final week, and the split moves from Cold to Hot.
  • The money comes out of the show’s prospecting, not on top.

What stands from the 17 Nov records: the panel’s layout and columns, Accept and Reject, the purchase read and its caveat, RT6’s payday window, overlaps taking the higher stretch (RT11), and the Purchase create path.

Rulings

#Ruling
RF1 · Cold, Warm, Hot
  • The tiers are named Cold, Warm and Hot on screen and in Meta, replacing Mid, Mid Bottom and Bottom.
  • The campaign is “{code} · Retargeting”, and its ad sets are “{code} · Retargeting · Cold”, “… · Warm” and “… · Hot”.
RF2 · One window per source
  • The longer window covers the shorter, and campaigns run about 8 weeks. RT14’s three windows per source go.
  • Cold: video viewers 75 %, 180 days (Matas, 26 Nov) · page and Instagram engagers, 180 days · followers, all time. Videos, engagers and followers come from the promoter’s and the lineup artists’ Facebook and Instagram accounts.
  • Warm: show page and ticket-link visitors, 60 days · engagers, 60 days · video viewers 95 %, 60 days (Matas, 26 Nov). A full watch without a click is interest, not intent.
  • Hot: ticket-link visitors, 30 days · checkout starters (one audience) · registrations and the signup list · engagers, 30 days · similar lists.
  • Leaves out: Cold leaves out Warm, Hot and buyers; Warm leaves out Hot and buyers; Hot leaves out buyers. Buyers get their own upsell campaign, in a later record.
  • Each audience row in a tier reads as the source and its window, with “{n} accounts” where the source is accounts.
RF3 · Shares and split
  • Replacing RT11 and RT2’s 50 / 25 / 25:

    Announce, first 2 weeks25 %60 %30 %10 %
    Normally30 %40 %40 %20 %

  • Overlaps take the higher stretch. Payday weeks come round inside “normally”, in RT6’s window.
  • The panel’s first line is “£{x} a day · {p} % of the show’s daily spend · {stretch}”. The footer names the next stretch, its date, its share and what it moves.
  • Each step is a scheduled budget change (MB2), so it shows on Today and can be cancelled there or in Claude.
RF4 · Where the money comes from
  • Retargeting is part of the show’s daily spend, not on top. The share is taken from the show’s prospecting budgets, largest first, never leaving an ad set under £1.00. A budget set on a prospecting campaign counts as one budget, and every ad set it feeds keeps £1.00.
  • The Create line says it: “Takes £{x} a day from prospecting, largest first: {name} £{a} → £{b} · … The show stays at £{s} a day.”
  • When prospecting can’t give it all (Matas, 26 Nov: take what’s there), retargeting starts at what it can give, and the line says so. With nothing to give, there’s no Create; the line says to raise the show’s budget first.
  • A paused build moves nothing. The money moves when it goes live, and each stretch step moves money between prospecting and retargeting the same way.
  • The ceiling checks the change in the show’s total. Taking from prospecting leaves it the same, so a full take starts no new spend.
RF5 · The frequency ceiling
  • Each tier’s share is a ceiling. When a tier’s 7-day frequency is over 3, it’s capped at what it spent a day over those 7 days. The rest goes to the next colder tier, and from Cold back to the prospecting budget it came from.
  • The tier row says it under Budget, in ochre: “Capped at £{c} a day: frequency {f} over 7 days. £{x} a day went to {tier}.”, then, muted, “It gets its share back once frequency is 3 or under.”
  • This replaces the 17 Nov HOLD at frequency 4.
RF6 · Missing advertiser access
  • Followers, engagers and video viewers need advertiser access to the promoter’s and artists’ accounts. Where an account is missing, the tier still builds without it.
  • Every tier has an account-based source, so the panel says it once, under the share line, in brick, with a one-form: each account by name and platform, and who to ask. audience_sources lists the same accounts for Claude.
RF7 · The lineup ad set goes
  • Cold retargeting replaces the separate lineup ad set in the usual campaigns. The broad Advantage+ ad set takes the lineup artists as interest suggestions, so their fans who haven’t engaged are still reached.
  • Campaign creator, Audiences: no lineup ad set. A muted line says why, where it used to be. Broad’s row lists the artists as suggestions and says how many are Meta interests.
  • Audience templates: a template’s lineup part becomes Broad’s suggestions the same way. The count in the template tab drops the lineup ad set.
  • A draft that already has a lineup ad set keeps it until Matas acts. Its row says, in ochre, “Cold retargeting replaces this ad set. Move its artists to Broad’s suggestions and remove it?” with Move to Broad and Keep. Published campaigns aren’t touched.
RF8 · The £1.00 floor
  • Meta’s £1.00 a day floor per tier stays. A tier whose share is under it gets £1.00, taken from the largest other tier, and its row says so, muted.
RF9 · Told to Claude: the plan and the rollout
  • plan_retargeting: one line per show, giving:
    • the stretch, the share, and £ a day per tier;
    • where the money comes from;
    • audiences made, to make, and missing access.
    Then up to three short lines: prospecting short, which accounts need access, and the next stretch as a scheduled change.
  • plan_retargeting("live") opens with the counts: to build and skipped. Skipped shows follow, one line each with the reason: no pixel, no ticket link, show within 3 days, archived client, already built, or nothing to take.
  • create_retargeting: answers with what it made, paused or live, and when the money moves.

Acceptance

  • Tiers and audiences: Meta names exactly as RF1, one audience per source and window as RF2, the exclusions exactly as RF2. A test checks each tier’s include and exclude lists.
  • Shares: the plan matches RF3’s table for every stretch on fixtures, overlaps take the higher, and every step is a scheduled change.
  • Money: a full take leaves the show’s total unchanged; no prospecting ad set goes under £1.00; a short take builds at what it got and says so; a paused build moves nothing.
  • Ceiling: a tier over frequency 3 is capped at its 7-day average spend; the extra goes colder, then back to the budget it came from; it lifts at 3 or under.
  • Access: a missing account leaves its sources out and every tier still builds.
  • Lineup: no new draft makes a lineup ad set; an old one asks before it moves; published campaigns are untouched.
  • Words: every string is a row, exactly; nothing raw from Meta.

Invented here

  • Every word above that isn’t the 17 Nov records’ or Matas’s.
  • Rules:
    • a campaign budget counts as one prospecting budget, each ad set under it keeping £1.00;
    • a paused build moves no money;
    • the cap sits at the tier’s 7-day average spend, and its extra goes back to the budget it came from;
    • the floor’s top-up comes from the largest other tier;
    • missing access said once per panel, not per tier;
    • an old draft’s lineup ad set asks before it moves.
  • Frame contents: Rudimental’s money, ad sets, support artists and every figure; the XMPL shows.
