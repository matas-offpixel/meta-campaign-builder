The on-sale refresh: one proposal per campaign, accepted line by line

Matas, 12 Nov: once a show is on sale, the remaining budget should run to the end of the campaign, optimising for purchases. It gets there through an Initiate Checkout (IC) or Landing Page View (LPV) campaign, whichever performs best. IC is preferred, but only where the pixel's IC fires; elsewhere it's LPV. A Purchase campaign runs alongside, and budget moves between campaigns by best-performing audience, as he did by hand for Ironworks. Creatives and audiences refresh from what the data says. A condensed version comes to presale later: on-sale first, then condense it.

This record rules the loop that turns the existing learning into a proposal Matas accepts or rejects line by line. These already exist and are not re-ruled here:

  • ad_daily_insights and the nightly learning-refresh (creative_scores, tag_performance, copy performance, interest_clusters.live_evidence, client_funnel_benchmarks);
  • the 4-hourly optimisation tick (evaluate → apply) and its gates, floors and pause limits;
  • the pacing alerts;
  • the fortnightly payday-aligned cycle and its Drive folders;
  • the ticket-warning ladder;
  • From Drive (#1200);
  • the gated Meta write paths.

Grounded in lib/optimisation/apply.ts, lib/learning/read.ts, lib/creative-lab/payday.ts and lib/drive/tree.ts, and in a read of P26-BDAY's rows taken 12 Nov (data to Wed 11 Nov).

Rulings

#Ruling
R1 Cadence

The proposal is accepted. Two triggers, both Mondays and both ready by 08:00 London, after the nightly learning-refresh and inside Matas's Monday planning:

  • Cycle refresh, on each on-sale cycle's Monday: the payday-week Monday and the Monday two weeks after it. It covers creatives and claims, plus everything the mid-cycle check covers.
  • Mid-cycle check, on the Monday between: budget and audiences only, with no creative adds.

The data window is the last full Monday–Sunday week, which is how Matas judges rounds. Significance is also read cumulatively since on sale, from creative_scores.

Data sets the floor. If a campaign logged fewer than 20 results in the week, its proposal holds no pauses, drops or moves. It carries only supply (adds and claims) and says Not enough data this week — next check Mon {d Mon}.

No other triggers: nothing daily and no proposal per budget alert. Pacing alerts and the 4-hourly budget tick carry on as they are.

Show week: from 4 Show week's Monday the refresh stops, and the show-week burst takes over.

Shadow catch-up: a shadow run may be started by hand for any day. It reads the data to the day before.

R2 The proposal

One proposal per campaign per refresh. Each line has a kind, a target, its evidence and what it would write. The kinds:

  • Pause creative.
    • Qualifies when all of these hold: its convert-axis score is significant and in the campaign's bottom third; it has been live at least 7 days; it has spent at least 2× the campaign's cost per result.
    • Never the last delivering ad in an ad set, and at most 3 per campaign per refresh.
    • Evidence: ad copies (n), spend, results, cost per result against the campaign's, the score and whether it's significant.
    • Watch list: creatives past the cost bar but not yet significant are shown under Watch, with no action.
  • Add creative. Approved finals in this cycle's owner folders ({owner} / Videos | Statics / UGC | CTA | No CTA) that aren't already in the campaign, matched by drive_file_id.
    • The mix follows the cycle. The payday cycle is CTA-led: 2 of every 3 adds from CTA folders, plus approved ticket warnings. The content cycle (weeks 3–4) is UGC- and No CTA-led: 2 of every 3 from those folders.
    • Limits: at most 6 adds per campaign per refresh, each going into every ad set that is delivering.
  • Claim ready, no creative. An approved ticket-warning claim with no render yet points to the Lab. It's a supply line, not a Meta write.
  • Bring back an audience. Qualifies for a stopped ad set of this event with at least 10 results at no more than 0.6× the campaign's cost per result. Also for a client interest cluster whose live evidence has a shrunk index of at least 1.2, confidence at least 0.5, and isn't used on this event.
  • Missing standing default: a retargeting ad set, or the registrations lookalike in its own ad set.
  • Drop an audience. Qualifies for a delivering ad set that has run at least 7 days, spent at least 3× the campaign's cost per result, and runs at at least 2× it. Never the last ad set, never retargeting.
  • Move budget (R3, R5).

Lines are ordered by expected effect, and each one says plainly why. A rule with a poor track record (R6) sorts last.

R3 Objective choice

IC is chosen when the event's own campaigns logged at least 10 IC in the last 14 days (ad_daily_insights actions). For an event with no history yet, it's chosen when the pixel's own event counts show at least 10 InitiateCheckout in 7 days; that's a new read-only Meta call. Otherwise the event runs LPV.

IC is preferred whenever it fires. LPV is chosen only where it doesn't. It isn't chosen on cost, because LPV is always cheaper per result and that says nothing about intent.

Purchase. A Purchase campaign is proposed only when the pixel logged at least 15 purchases in 14 days, the existing pause conversion minimum. When the event has a ticketing link, the line also shows the pixel's share of real sales.

Moves between campaigns need that purchase signal. With it, campaigns and audiences compare on cost per purchase (the Ironworks move). Without it, there are no cross-campaign moves, only within a campaign by its own cost per result.

The why line is always shown at the top of the proposal, for example Initiate Checkout — 212 reached the pixel since 21 Oct. Purchase campaign not proposed — 1 purchase on the pixel in 3 weeks.

R4 Approval and apply

Where it lives. On the event page, as a Refresh panel (F1, F2). The MML canvas shows only a count chip on the campaign node (Refresh · 4 to review) that opens the event page. A Slack post in ads_automation links to it when a proposal is ready.

Accept and Apply. Every line is unset until Matas chooses Accept or Reject; Reject asks for an optional reason. Apply accepted (n) writes only the accepted lines, each through its existing path:

  • Add creative: the launch path's ad creation into the existing ad sets, with the files copied by #1200's path.
  • Bring back: the ad set's status, via a new gated status write, or a new ad set through the launch path.
  • Audience edits: adset-targeting-write.
  • Ad-set budget: the optimisation budget write.
  • Moves between lifetime campaigns: a new gated lifetime_budget write.
  • Pause creative: a new gated ad-status write.

Ads go in live, into ad sets that are already delivering. The Accept click is the approval, and the line shows the creative and copy before it's clicked. Paused ads tend to be forgotten, and Meta's review still applies. New ad sets also go in live, but only when their budget comes from a named move or plan headroom (R5).

Line states: To review · Accepted · Rejected · Applied · Failed — {reason} · Expired. Unanswered lines expire at the next refresh.

Shadow mode is the default. Proposals are generated, stored and shown read-only, marked Shadow, and each line is scored next week (R6).

Arming. Live mode needs a global gate plus a per-event arm (Shadow / Live), as the budget tick does. Nothing applies without Matas's click. Applying without a click isn't in this record.

R5 Guardrails
  • Spend never goes above plan. The plan is the event's marketing budget minus spend to date. With no budget set, a line that adds spend is shown but can't be accepted, and reads Set this event's budget to accept this.
  • Moves are capped. At most 20 % of the remaining budget moves in one refresh.
  • Live ads are never edited. Their creative and copy stay as they are; changes arrive only as new ads.
  • Existing limits apply. The pause floor, the 15-conversion pause minimum, max writes per run and never pausing the last ad set all apply, and nothing auto-resumes.
  • Claims. They come only from ticket-warning rows Matas approved, live in their window. The refresh never writes a claim and never words scarcity itself.
  • Where to write. Only to campaigns of this event's drafts on this account.
R6 Learning from its own moves

What's recorded. Every line (shadow, rejected or applied) is stored with its evidence snapshot.

How it's judged. A new nightly learning-refresh job scores each line on the next full week:

  • Pause: right if the campaign's cost per result held or improved, and the creative had kept running worse than 1.5× the campaign.
  • Add: right if the new ad's convert score is at or above the campaign median after 7 days.
  • Bring back or drop: right if the audience's cost per result came in on the predicted side of the campaign's.
  • Move: right if the receiving side held its cost per result within 20 %.

Track record. Each rule × client has a hit rate, used from 10 outcomes. Under 40 % sorts the rule's lines last and marks them low track record. Under 25 % over 20 outcomes stops proposing that rule for the client, and the panel tells Matas.

Rejections with the same reason 5 times in a row for a client do the same.

R7 Scope

Meta only in this record.

  • TikTok comes next, after Meta's piece 3. It needs its rollup sync fixed first (failing on 31 events this week), plus ad-status and budget writes; its CompletePayment and InitiateCheckout checks are to be ruled. The launch path exists.
  • Google gets report-only lines later, from google-ads-daily-insights. There are no Google writes.
R8 Presale, noted only

Same loop, different parameters. The build keeps one phase config, so nothing assumes weekly. Its fields:

  • cadence: every 2 days at 08:00 on presale;
  • window: a rolling 7–10 days, not Monday–Sunday;
  • resultEvent: CompleteRegistration on presale, not IC;
  • minDays and minResults: smaller on presale, leaning on the existing shrinkage and showing confidence on every line;
  • maxPauses;
  • supplyFolders: 1 Signup / Teaser and Announcement on presale;
  • claimsLadder: the presale rows on presale.

Presale has no Purchase campaign.
