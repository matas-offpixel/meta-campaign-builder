Rulings

#Ruling
O37 The claim's entrance
  • The name, the details, the lockup (where the template has one) and the bands are on from frame 0: the artist is the hook, and the first frame is often the thumbnail.
  • The claim enters at 1.5 s with a 0.25 s opacity fade (linear, 0 → 100 %), no movement, and holds to the last frame. There is no exit; on a loop the restart hides it again until 1.5 s.
  • Minimum on screen: 3.0 s. A clip shorter than 4.75 s takes no claim: "Clip too short for a claim — needs 4.75 s". It can still run as T1 without one.
  • A claim never changes mid-clip; between rungs only the claim changes, the cut stays (O3).
O38 The bands on videoThe bands enter with the clip at frame 0 and never move or change. The top band is O25's (full to 0.44 H, gone by 0.53 H). The bottom band follows the claim (O43): zero at 0.55 H, J2's fade to 20 % at 0.60 H, rising evenly to 30 % at 0.68 H and held to the bottom edge — so it also sits under the platforms' own caption and controls. 0.53–0.55 H is clear. In the 4:5 and 1:1 the bands are carried as the type is (O25).
O39 The face on clipsJudged by time in the box, not a band: clips aren't cropped to a face.
  • Sampled at 5 frames a second. In each sample, the main face's box (faces under half its width don't count, O19) is tested against the top block (name and details) and the claim box, each padded 12 px.
  • Limit: the face touches type in no more than 10 % of the samples while that type is on screen, and never for more than 0.5 s in a row.
  • Over the limit, the crop (O41) is tried first; if no crop passes, the clip takes no T1: "Face in the type for {n} % of the claim's time — choose another crop or clip".
  • Face size and the stills' face band (O18, O23) don't apply to clips.
O40 Contrast on clipsO19's tests, per frame, on the same 5-a-second samples with the bands on and before any type; the clip's figure is the 95th percentile (nearest rank) across the samples in which that type is on screen. Limits as on stills: the top block ≤ 10 % below 3:1, the claim box ≤ 15 % below 3:1, the red line's ink box ≤ 25 % below 2:1. The red fallback is decided per clip: if the red line fails, line 1 is white for the whole clip and never switches mid-clip; the approval line is the stills' ("claim line 1 in white — red unreadable on this photo", with "clip" for "photo").
O41 crop_focus_yThe crop moves the footage, never the type. Slots, bands and the safe area are fixed to the output frame; crop_focus_y chooses where the footage sits under them, per format. The crop picker (B2) draws the top block, the claim box and the safe area on its preview, so the crop can be chosen to keep the face out of the type (O39). (This corrects the issue-5 leaning that the bands follow it.)
O42 The 1:1 for clipsA crop: the footage covers 1080 × 1080, centred on the 1:1's own crop_focus_y, with the type at O1's 0.8× re-sit. No pillarbox, no blurred fill, no side extension — O20 extends stills, never video. No upscaling: a 1080-wide 9:16 source gives a 1080 × 1080 window.
O43 The video safe area and the claim
  • The 9:16 video safe area is the larger margin of the two platforms: x 120–960 (0.111–0.889 W), y 269–1248 (0.14–0.65 H). Every T1 type slot on a 9:16 clip sits inside it.
  • O3's claim zone does not hold on clips. On a clip the claim is bottom-anchored on 0.65 H (y 1248): one line at O8's 4 % H (77 px) has its cap top at 0.610 H; two lines at 4 % H with O4's pitch ratio (0.0583 H) put line 1's cap top at 0.552 H; step 2 (3.4 % H) scales the same way. Width ≤ 840 px (0.778 W), centred.
  • The top block keeps its static slots (O3, with O18's 76 px inset), all inside the safe area; the name and details also keep within 840 px.
  • The middle, 0.44 H to the claim's top, carries no type (O3).
  • TikTok's engagement rail: one guide widens the right margin to 300 px below y 840. A centred claim can't honour that and stay centred; H44's preview check decides whether it overlaps the icons.
  • 4:5 and 1:1 video (feed placements, no Reels controls over the media) take the same arrangement, carried by the crop and the 0.8× re-sit (O1, O2): the claim's last line on 0.713 of the rectangle's height.
O44 A caption and T1A clip carries either a UGC caption (rule 13, the Lab's rounds) or T1's type (name, details, claim, from the asset board) — never both, with or without a claim. The caption's block sits at about 36 % H, inside T1's details block. Neither yields: they are two different ads.
O45 The kit-font licenceO24 holds: kit type renders only in the kit's own faces, and no server render (Lambda, or the CI runner) uses a face until its licence is recorded for that use. Local drafts on Matas's machine are unchanged. Until Bootzy's licence is recorded, no T1 clip renders and T2's cards don't become CI frames (#1163's follow-up).
  • Per face, on the kit. The Brand kit component's font row gains a licence line: "Licence · Add proof" until recorded.
  • What counts as proof: a document, not a typed statement — the licence certificate or EULA naming the uses, or the foundry's written confirmation (an email). Matas uploads it; it is filed in {Client} / _Brand kit / Licences.
  • The uses ticked with it, by Matas: server rendering (needed for Lambda and CI) and video ads (needed for T1 clips). Desktop-only licences usually exclude server rendering; the Lab doesn't judge the document's legal reach, Matas does.
  • Recorded: "Licence recorded · server · video · {who}, {d Mon}". A kit whose face lacks a use holds the cards that need it: "Held — {face}: licence for server rendering not recorded".
O46 Dithering the bandsThe band layer is computed at 16 bits and dithered to 8 bits with a fixed blue-noise pattern (±0.5 level, the same pattern every frame, so it doesn't crawl), then encoded at H.264 CRF ≤ 18. Acceptance on the encoded file over a flat mid-grey test clip: across each fade, no step between adjacent rows' mean luma larger than 2 levels.
O47 The seam methodO20 (c), the seam after an extension, measured: on the photo layer with the extension and the bands, before any type or lockup; per column, the mean colour of the 8 px rows either side of the join; ΔE is CIE76 on CIELAB from sRGB under D65 (colour.ts); the 95th percentile across columns by nearest rank; ≤ 2 passes. The build's figure is the one of record (Enzo 1: 1.45).
O48 O28 on T1, and release namesO28's line-break limits apply to T1 (ruled on #1163, logged here): never inside a release name — any word followed by RELEASE, EARLY BIRD, GENERAL SALE, the event's own release names — and never between FINAL and its number. T1's planClaim passes the event's own release names (from event_releases) to the shared break rule, as T2 does, so a two-word name like "Early entry" holds together.
Open, for Matas: the 9:16 static's placement. The artist card's claim (0.736–0.812 H) and T2's lower corners sit inside Reels' bottom 35 %. That is fine where the 9:16 static runs in Stories, where the guides give the bottom margin as 14 % (one says Instagram Stories draws 35 %, like Reels). The recommendation: the 9:16 static runs in Stories placements, and the first one is checked in Ads Manager's Stories and Reels previews (H44). If it must run in Reels, the static needs the video claim position — a later issue.
