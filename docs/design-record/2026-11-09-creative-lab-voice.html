Creative Lab — voice: the writer learns tone from the page, not from a generic style

The caption writer today shows every client the same seed examples (NX Loves and Junction 2 lines in corpus.ts) and asks the model to copy their shapes. That is why the output reads as one agency voice, and sometimes as a machine's. This record rules a voice corpus: per page — the client's promoter Instagram and each artist's Instagram — the page's own last N captions, read from the edge Find clips already reads, stored verbatim, and handed to the writer as exemplars of tone. The validator (caption-facts.ts, A11 as built) is untouched; copy_features still supplies the shape; the corpus supplies the voice. Frames at 1280 and 393; every value not read from the code or named by Matas is listed under Invented.

"we should take learnings for suggestions from the artist or promoter IG profile captions to be more on brand and less generic / clearly AI written. Content needs to feel genuine and authentic to resonate." — Matas, 8 Nov 2026

V · Rulings

#Ruling
V1 What the corpus isPer page — the client's promoter Instagram (clients.instagram_business_id) and each artist's Instagram (creative_lab_artists) — the page's own last N captions, N = 30 (Invented, flagged), by posted date. Each is stored verbatim with its permalink, posted date, likes and comments; nothing is trimmed, corrected or de-emojied. The corpus is exemplars of tone: how the page writes, not what it has written. Reels, images and carousels count alike; a post with no caption is skipped and does not count toward N.
V2 Where it is readThe same media edge the Lab's discovery already reads: caption is added to IG_MEDIA_FIELDS, so the one call that ranks an artist's reels also carries their captions (own edge for an artist with instagram_actor_id; Business Discovery by ig_username otherwise). The promoter page gains the own-edge read it does not have today — /{instagram_business_id}/media with the same fields, through the same token. No new permission, no new token. media_url stays absent; a caption is public text the page published.
V3 Where the voice landsBoth layers, with the overlay's own rules intact. The overlay (burned text) takes sentence length, case, punctuation habit, and how the page names the venue and the lineup. Emoji, sign-offs and hashtags are habits of the primary text only — the overlay has no emoji, no #, not all capitals, as today. Hashtags are read and shown as a trait but not written into an ad unless Matas turns it on per client (creative_lab_style_settings.hashtags_in_primary, default off — proposed, flagged): a hashtag in paid primary text is a link off the ad. Mentions (@) are read and never written.
V4 Shape, voice, facts — three sources, one promptThe prompt has three blocks and each has one owner. Shape: the style's seed examples (corpus.ts, unchanged) and, where the rail has a winner for the stage and scope (R20), the copy_features finding as the recommended angle — the generator's pre-set (R23), nothing more. Voice: the page's exemplars and the traits the extractor took from them (V11). Facts: the event record through the tokens, as today. The seed lines keep their job ("copy the shape, not the facts"); the exemplars take the job the seed lines were never meant to do. When the corpus has fewer than M = 8 exemplars (Invented), the voice block is omitted and the chip says so (V8); the prompt is then exactly today's.
V5 Which page feeds whichvoiceForStyle decides: artist_first_person reads the artist's page (the artist picked on the generator, or the clip's artist); every other style reads the promoter's. Never both in one call; never another client's. Quote mode is untouched — a quote is the artist's own words and nothing is written.
V6 RefreshOn Find clips (the same call, no second request) and nightly in clip-sync's Instagram pass, where the promoter page is added as one more read per client. New posts upsert on (page, ig_media_id); likes and comments update on every read; nothing is deleted — the prompt takes the N most recent by posted date. The sheet shows when the page was last read. A read error lands on the artist's ig_last_error (or a new clients.ig_last_error, Invented) and on the chip (V8).
V7 Never lines to copyExemplars are tone. A candidate that repeats six or more consecutive words (Invented) of any exemplar is dropped by the writer's dedupe with code voice_copy, visible through onReject like any other drop. One exception, for Matas to confirm: a recurring sign-off — the same closing line in 3 or more of the N posts — may close the primary text verbatim, because it is the page's own refrain and the page is the advertiser. On the artist's page the refrain is the artist's and takes the artist-channel gate as any artist-voice caption does (B1).
V8 States — the chip always says which voiceOne component, the voice chip, under the generator row, in caption mode: Sounds like @puzzle · 24 posts› when the corpus has ≥ M exemplars; otherwise a dashed House voice chip with the reason: No posts read yet — Find clips reads them (Matas's sentence, verbatim) · @handle has 5 posts, fewer than 8 · @handle can't be read: not a business or creator account (Meta's reason through plainIgMessage, rule 7) · Add Instagram (no handle; the artist row's own action). House voice is today's prompt, named, never a silent fallback. The chip opens the sheet (V10); its count is posts read, not posts on the page.
V9 The validator is unchangedcaption-facts.ts runs after the writer exactly as today, A11 included (A12): scarcity words need the ticket data or a live approved claim; dates, venue, lineup come from tokens; the artist-voice checks stand. An exemplar may carry a past fact ("27.12", "last 50") — the prompt's standing instruction (tokens, never a typed fact) and the validator catch it, as they catch the seed lines' facts today. Voice never relaxes a fact and a trait never introduces one.
V10 The sheetOpens from the chip, right side at 1280 (440 px), bottom sheet at 393. Header: the handle, posts read, when, and the cadence ("refreshed on Find clips and nightly"). Then Traits the writer took — each trait with its value and its evidence as n of N (V11), a trait that is read but not written marked so. Then Exemplars — the captions verbatim with date and likes, the permalink behind a ↗ (rule 5: no ids, no URL text). Footer: "Exemplars set the tone. No line is reused." One action: Read again (Find clips). Nothing on the sheet is editable; the corpus is read, never curated.
V11 TraitsThe extractor is a pure function, samples in → traits out, deterministic, with a count of N behind every trait (Invented list, flagged): post length (median characters, band), sentences per post, words per sentence (median and range), case habit (sentence · lower · caps share), emoji (set by frequency, posts carrying any, position lead · inline · trail), hashtags (posts carrying any, median count, trailing block or inline), mentions (posts carrying any), punctuation (exclamation and question share, em dash, ellipsis), line breaks (median lines), sign-off (a closing line recurring in ≥ 3), venue naming (the forms the venue appears in, by count — "Circuit" 11 · "Circuit Southampton" 0), lineup naming (separator: new line · "/" · "b2b" · comma; "& more" habit). The prompt receives the traits as sentences the model can follow, not as JSON (HV2).
V12 Who sees whatA page's captions are public posts; the corpus is per page and never pooled. A client's promoter corpus shows to that client and to Matas; an artist's corpus shows wherever the artist row shows. Nothing from one client's page reaches another client's writer (rule 12 holds without a 🔒 line, because there is no cross-client read). Removing an artist row removes its samples (Invented: cascade).
V13 393The chip drops under the generator's Write row, full width, the count on the right. The sheet is a bottom sheet at 88 %: traits first, exemplars under them, Read again at the foot. The can't-read and empty sentences stack under the chip.

CLV1 · The voice chip on the generator (1280 and 393)

The chip is a statement, not an input (rule 3 is unchanged: the visible inputs are the generator's). It names the page the writer is about to sound like and how many of its posts it has read; the why line says which voice the current angle takes and where the other two blocks of the prompt come from. Switching the angle to Artist, first person switches the chip to the artist's page — nothing else on the row changes. The handle is read from the page, never typed.

CLV2 · The voice sheet — exemplars and the traits the writer took (1280 and 393)

The sheet shows the writer's working, not a settings page. Every trait carries the count that justifies it, so a thin habit (two exclamations in twenty-four posts) reads as thin. Two traits are drawn with their value and a note that they are read and not written (V3): the hashtag block and the mentions. The exemplars are the posts as posted, dated, with likes, and the post itself behind the icon. There is nothing to edit here: the corpus is what the page wrote.

CLV3 · The empty state — no posts read yet (1280 and 393)

CLV4 · An artist whose feed can't be read, or is empty (1280 and 393)

Three failures, one component, each saying what unlocks it (rule 8): a private or personal account (Meta's reason, in a sentence), a feed with too few posts (the count and the floor), a row with no handle (the row's own action). None of them blocks writing — the house voice is today's writer — and none of them is silent. The stale case is not a failure: the corpus stands and the sheet dates it.

The prompt contract (HV2) — what goes in, what comes out

The writer's system prompt gains one block between the shape examples and the facts. It is prose the model can follow, generated from the traits, and then the exemplars verbatim. The facts block and the tool are unchanged; the output is still submit_captions candidates with the tokens in place.

VOICE — @puzzle (promoter). These are the page's own recent posts. Write in this voice. Use them for tone only: never reuse a sentence or six consecutive words from any of them. Traits (of 24 posts): - Posts run 2–3 short sentences, about 140 characters; sentences average 7 words (4–12). - Sentence case; never all capitals. - Emoji: 🖤 🔥 👀, one per post, at the end of a line — in the primary text only; the overlay has none. - A recurring sign-off, "See you on the floor 🖤" (9 of 24), may close the primary text as written. - The venue is "Circuit", never "Circuit Southampton" — write {venue} and the record fills it. - Lineups go on their own lines, no "/", "& more" when long — write {lineup}. - Full stops; rarely an exclamation; no em dash. - The page uses hashtags and @mentions; do not write either. Exemplars, newest first: 1. Puzzle turns 4. Lineup drops Monday 👀 / Set your alarms 2. Sold out. Thank you Southampton 🖤 / See you on the floor 🖤 3. Last 50 for Saturday. Don't say we didn't tell you 🔥 / Link in bio … Facts still come only from the tokens. Never type a date, a venue, a price or a lineup from an exemplar.

Shape stays where it is: the style's seed examples and, where the rail has a winner, the angle pre-set. Facts stay where they are: the tokens and the validator. The voice block is the only addition, and it is omitted whole when the corpus is under the floor (V4).
