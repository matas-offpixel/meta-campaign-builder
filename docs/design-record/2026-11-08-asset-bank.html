Asset bank — releases, the sold count, and warnings that are true

Extends pass 2's B5 (the board, the payday calendar, the approval card) with three facts from Matas: an event runs several releases, not one presale and one general sale; tickets sold per release are entered by hand, because Matas has no access to the clients' Skiddle or Ironworks dashboards; and every ticket-warning asset is grounded in those two things, held for his Approve, and timed around payday. Rules 6, 8, 11 and 14 apply throughout. Numbers in frames are placeholders unless the data note fixes them.

A · Rulings

#Ruling
A1 ReleasesAn event has an ordered list of releases: name, community time, list time, price, capacity. Community time is when the WhatsApp community gets the link; list time is when signups do (and the public, for general sale). A release with no community time is public. events.presale_at = the first release's community time; events.general_sale_at = the general-sale release's list time; both derived, never typed twice.
A2 The timeline is one lineReleases draw on one horizontal line with today and the next payday marked. Each release is a dot with its name above and its price below. Capacity and sold are not on the line; they're in the sold bars underneath. One line, however many releases.
A3 Sold by hand, one stepTickets sold are entered on the sold bars: one bar per release, length = capacity, filled to sold. Matas drags the handle or taps the bar at the count; the number updates as he drags, and the entry saves with today's date, his name and the source he picks once per event (Skiddle screenshot, client message, Ironworks). Never a form, never a table of fields. The total (1,240 of 2,000 · 62%) is the biggest number on the panel. An entry can only move a count forward; a correction downwards asks once ("Lower than last time, 1,310 on 5 Nov. Sure?").
A4 Freshness is always shownEvery sold count carries its entry date. Older than 3 days it reads in ochre with entered Tue 4 Nov · update?; older than 7 days the warnings that depend on it lock (A5), because a warning built on a stale count is a fabricated one.
A5 Warnings and their truth conditionsSix warning assets, each grounded in the timeline and the sold count. Presale live: a release's community or list time has passed and it has capacity left. General sale live: the general-sale release's list time has passed. Final 100: remaining across all releases ≤ 100 (the threshold is the asset's name; a Final 50 variant follows the same rule). Price rise on {date}: the next release has a higher price and a list time; {date} is that list time's day. 80% sold: sold ÷ total capacity ≥ 0.80. Sold out: sold = total capacity. A warning whose condition isn't met is locked and says how far off it is; it has no Approve.
A6 Approve, then it's scheduled, then it runsEvery warning is an approval card: artwork preview, the sentence it says, the number that makes it true with its source and entry date, and when it will run. Approve → Scheduled → Live. Nothing runs without Approve. Not now snoozes to the next payday window. An approved warning whose condition stops being true before it runs (a release extended, a count corrected) goes back to Held and tells Today.
A7 TimingTicket warnings run inside the payday window (code: Thursday before the last Friday, 18 days). Content-led pushes (artist cards, UGC, experience) run mid-month, outside the window. Presale live and General sale live are the exceptions: they run at their release time whatever the calendar says. Price rise on {date} runs from 4 days before the date to the date, and if that overlaps a payday window it starts on the window's first day.
A8 Payday exceptionsWhen the last Friday is a bank holiday or 24–26 December, the payday push moves to the previous working day and the calendar says why on the day. No other movement; the rule stays last Friday.
A9 The board tells the truth about what's missingEvery ticket-warning cell on the board is one of: a thumbnail (made), a thumbnail with ✓ (approved), held in ochre (true, waiting for Approve), locked dashed (not true yet, with the count that unlocks it), or + needs … dashed (true but can't be made: no PSD, no font, no artwork). Make the missing n makes only the cells that are true and makeable.
A10 393The timeline stays one line and scrolls sideways if the names overlap. Sold bars stack full width; the handle is 32 px tall for a thumb. Approval cards: preview left, words right, buttons on their own row. The calendar is a list by week. The board shows two formats at a time and swipes.

AB1 · Release timeline and the sold count — on the event page, above the board (1280 and 393)

The dashed tick on the general-sale bar is where 80% sold becomes true for the whole event (1,600), drawn on the bar that will cross it. The handle is the only control: drag it, or tap the bar where the count is. The source line is the provenance (rule 5 says no raw ids; rule 11 says show where the number came from). When a provider sync exists for the client, the bars fill themselves and the source line reads Eventbrite · read 09:00; the hand entry stays available for corrections.

AB2 · Approval cards — held, locked, and one that went back (1280 and 393)

Every card shows the number that makes it true, where it came from and when it was entered. The fourth card is the rule in A6 drawn: an approved warning whose number changed goes back to Held with the new render, and says in one sentence what changed. It never runs the old artwork.
