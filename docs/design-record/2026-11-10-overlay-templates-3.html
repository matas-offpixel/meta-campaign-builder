Overlay templates — issue 3: the claim on the artist card, gradient bands, the Instagram fallback, fonts

Extends issue 1 (2026-11-08-overlay-templates.html, O1–O17) and issue 2 (2026-11-10-overlay-templates-2.html, O18–O21). Both stand except where a row below says otherwise. Matas's direction of 10 Nov: cut-outs paused; work from the press photos, and failing those, from the artist's own Instagram; CTA edits on statics come before video, so the artist card gains its claim; a gradient shadow behind the overlay text; the claim only in the kit's font. The numbers come from the #1154 measurements of Puzzle overlay.psd, Puzzle overlay 2.psd and J2 2026 Launch Ad Overlay 9x16, and the matte evaluation merged as #1152.

Rulings added or amended in issue 3

#Ruling
O21 PausedVariant B (the cut-out on a plain ground) is paused, not built: cut-outs are rarely asked for, and #1152 found no matte that passes more than one photo in six. O21 stays on record as the rule a future B build is judged against; H17 is shelved. A photo that fails variant A is now skipped (O19's order becomes: press photos, then the Instagram fallback, each through A; then skip).
O19 Amended
  • One subject. Only faces at least half the width of the largest face count; the count of those must be 1. A face of any size inside the text box or the claim box fails the photo. This lets live photos with a crowd pass.
  • Scrim. "No scrim" becomes "no scrim beyond the kit gradient" (O25). The gradient is fixed per kit; it is never darkened, widened or moved for a photo to make it pass.
  • Contrast measured with the gradient on. The text-box test (≤ 10 % of pixels below 3:1 against white) is measured on the placed photo with O25's bands applied, before any type — the test reads what ships.
  • The claim box (O23: 0.07–0.93 W, from the claim's cap top to 0.812 H) gets the same test with the gradient on: ≤ 10 % below 3:1 against white.
  • The red line. In the rows of a kit-red line's cap box (0.07–0.93 W), gradient on: ≤ 10 % of pixels below 2:1 against #FE3232. If it fails, that line renders white — the words never change — and the approval line reads "claim line 1 in white — red unreadable on this photo".
O22 The Instagram photo fallbackWhen no press photo for the artist passes variant A, a still from the artist's own Instagram is tried, through A's gate unchanged.
  • Order. Press photos provided first; then Instagram, newest first; the first photo that passes is used.
  • Which posts. Photo posts only, each image of a carousel counting as one. Not reels or video stills, not stories, not tagged posts, not reposts. A collab post counts only when the artist's handle is a co-author. Not flyers or graphics: a post whose detected text covers more than 2 % of the image is out. Posted within the last 24 months. At most the last 50 photo posts are read.
  • How it's read. The handle is the one on the artist record that the voice corpus reads (V-rulings), through the same Instagram discovery read. Nothing is scraped. An account that can't be read (not a business or creator account) skips the fallback.
  • Rights — the 6 Nov ruling. An artist's assets and feed photos may run from the promoter's Page without the artist's approval; approval is needed only to run on the artist's own channels. So: from the promoter's Page (e.g. Puzzle), no gate — the card runs like any other artist asset, including for an artist whose chip reads "Promoter page only". From the artist's own channels, the existing gate (B1, pass 2) applies with no new words: the rights chip must read "✓ Cleared — artist channels too"; otherwise that version is Held with the chip's action "Clear for artist channels" beside it, and clearing releases it without a rebuild (as CLQ4).
  • The artist's own posts only, and the photographer credit carried over: if the caption credits a photographer, the credit is copied verbatim onto the card.
  • Identity. No face recognition, ever. The person approving the card checks that it is the right artist.
  • Size. Instagram serves at most 1080 × 1350 (or 1440 × 1800). That covers the 4:5 and the 1:1 at 1.0×; the 9:16 needs about 570 px added at the top, which passes only when O20 does. If the 9:16 fails, the card ships as 4:5 and 1:1, and its approval line says "9:16 not made — photo too short".
  • Disclosure. "photo from @{handle}'s Instagram, {d Mon yyyy}", followed by " · photo by {credit}" when a credit was carried over.
O23 The claim on the artist cardThe artist card gains its claim: T1 with the claim slot filled. O18's "claim slot empty" is amended to: the claim is filled when the event has an approved ladder claim that is true now (A12 and the asset-bank ladder — A5', A14 the roll-call rung, A15, A16); with none, the card renders as O18 drew it.
  • Words. Verbatim from the approved claim, line break and case as approved. No case transform, no edits, no claim without an approved rung behind it — no fabricated scarcity. A count ("FINAL 50 TICKETS") renders only when the approved rung carries that count.
  • This replaces O3 on the artist card. The #1154 builder placed the claim per O3 (the roll-call's red line at 0.7132 H, a two-line claim from ≈ 0.6965 H), above the chin limit. On the artist card O23's positions replace O3's. T1 video clips keep O3.
  • The top block (lockup, name, info) is unchanged from O18.
  • The face band. Head top ≥ 0.53 H; eyes 0.58–0.64 H; chin ≤ 0.72 H (was 0.76 H). With the 216 px face floor this needs head-top-to-chin ≥ 0.173 H (face box ≈ 0.65 × head-top-to-chin, measured on AC1–AC3). AC1–AC3 (chins 0.735–0.745 H) re-crop wider from source at ≈ 0.88×; where the source runs out, the sides extend per O20. Their face boxes become 221–246 px.
  • Sizes — the roll-call's own ratio. The roll-call keeps the PSD's sizes relative to the name, taken against the full name cap 0.1146 H (220 px), not a stepped-down name: line 1 kit red #FE3232 at 0.217 → cap 48 px; line 2 white at 0.347 → cap 76 px. Equal 63 px lines (the pre-ruling) are withdrawn. One-line claims take their piece's measured size and colour: the red "FINAL {n} TICKETS" at 0.556 → 122 px; the white "SELL OUT IMMINENT" at 0.479 → 105 px. A one-line claim with no piece in the kit is white at 0.479. Tracking +25 (the roll-call's, not the lineup's 5).
  • Position (9:16 master). The claim is bottom-anchored: its last line sits on 0.812 H (y 1559). Two-line: line 1 cap top y 1413 (0.736 H), 22 px gap, line 2 cap top y 1483 (0.772 H). That leaves 31 px under the 0.72 H chin. One-line: cap top y 1437 (red, 122 px) or y 1454 (white, 105 px). Centred, as the top block.
  • Fit. Each line within 80 % W (864 px). Too wide: all lines of the claim step down together, keeping their ratio, the bottom staying on 0.812 H. Floors: a two-line claim's line 1 ≥ 31 px (0.016 H, line 2 then 50 px); a one-line claim ≥ 50 px (0.026 H). Never condensed, never re-broken from two lines to three. Below the floor the card is held: "Claim too long for the artist card".
  • 4:5. A pure centre crop: the claim's last line ends 76 px above the 4:5's bottom edge, the same inset as the top.
  • 1:1. O1's re-sit at 0.8×: two-line caps 38 px and 61 px, line 1 cap top y 902, line 2 y 958, the last line 61 px above the bottom.
  • Shadow. O6 once on the whole text group, the claim included.
  • Contrast. O19's claim-box and red-line tests, gradient on.
O24 FontsKit text renders only in the kit's own fonts. No stand-in, ever. For Puzzle the name, info and claim are BootzyCondensedTM-Regular (found, #1154): the claim is no longer held. A kit with a missing font holds every card that uses it, with "Held — {font name} not installed". J2 is held now: "Held — PP Neue Machina not installed" (its ↙). GoodSans-Bold and AeroportMono are found and stay with the kits that name them.
O25 Gradient bands invented · judge on draftsMatas asked for a top and a bottom gradient shadow at about 20 % behind the overlay text, so names and claims hold on bright photos and video. No reference has one (data note), so these values are the builder's proposal, ruled as invented.
  • Layer. #000000, normal blend, 20 % opacity, two bands, full width, under all type and the lockup, over the photo (and over any O20 extension).
  • Top band. Full strength from the top edge to 0.44 H, then J2's fade shape to nothing at 0.53 H.
  • Bottom band. Full strength from 0.70 H to the bottom edge, fading upward to nothing at 0.64 H.
  • Fade shape. J2's mask normalised to the fade's length: 100 % → 90 % at 0.5 % of the fade, 50 % at 22.6 %, 10 % at 66.8 %, 0 at 100 %. Stops on the 9:16: top 100 % to 0.4400 H, 90 % at 0.4404, 50 % at 0.4603, 10 % at 0.5001, 0 at 0.5300; bottom 0 at 0.6400, 10 % at 0.6599, 50 % at 0.6864, 90 % at 0.6997, 100 % from 0.7000 H.
  • Never across the eyes. Both bands are zero from 0.53 H to 0.64 H, which holds the whole eyes band and the head top. The lower face (0.64–0.72 H) takes up to 20 % from the bottom band: accepted for the drafts; Matas judges it.
  • 4:5 and 1:1 — the bands move with the type. The bands serve the type, so they are carried exactly as the type is: the 4:5 crop and the 1:1's 0.8× re-sit (both run full width). Stops as fractions of the 4:5 and 1:1 height (the same in both, as 1350 × 0.8 = 1080): top 100 % to 0.4147, 90 % at 0.4153, 50 % at 0.4436, 10 % at 0.5001, 0 at 0.5427; bottom 0 at 0.6991, 10 % at 0.7274, 50 % at 0.7651, 90 % at 0.7840, 100 % from 0.7844. (This replaces the pre-ruling that anchored the bands to each format's own edges.)
  • Scope. The artist card and T1 on floating kits. T2 gets bands when its own draft is judged. Frame kits and T3 draw only what their own files hold — J2's cards keep their measured LEFT DARK LAYER, not these bands; its hidden DARK LAYER is not drawn.
  • O6 stays on the type, on top of the bands.
